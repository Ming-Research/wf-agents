### decision nothing-is-pushed-to-the-planner
Whoever plans the work learns what needs them by asking, with a call that waits until there is something; nothing is pushed to them.
Why: Pushed as it came, a message would break into what the assistant is discussing with the owner, whose context must stay on that. Asking again and again for the list of every item filled the asker's context as the items grew, and still found a work stopped at its limit only after the owner had.

### decision the-mark-is-a-place-in-the-records
The mark the planner keeps is a place in the records: the highest work there was, and for each work that had not ended the place up to which its records were read. Neither a number the running engine gives each change nor a time is used.
Why: A number given in memory begins again at every restart, and a reader that came back had to read everything again; a time is no place, since two works take things in the same millisecond and the clock can go back, so it would give a thing twice or not at all. Places in records only added to give the same answer to the same mark whatever happened between (principle state-from-records).

### decision what-stands-not-what-passed
A line is a work as it stands now, having come to stand so since the mark; what came and was settled between two calls, such as a question answered before the planner asked, gives none.
Why: The planner has nothing to do for what was settled, and every passing state listed would bury what needs them; this rests on judgment, with no case measured.

### decision the-owners-pause-is-no-line
A work the owner paused, or one added paused and not let on, is no line by itself; it shows as the reason a work waiting for its item is idle.
Why: Works are added paused by whoever plans them, who knows what they left so, and a line for each would repeat what they did; a work held up behind one is what they may not see. This rests on judgment.

### decision a-route-held-open-not-the-stream
The planner's call is a route of the interface that answers once, held open until there is something or the time asked for has passed, and not the stream of changes the page listens to.
Why: The stream says only which works grew and keeps no place that outlives the process, so its reader would work out again what is for the planner; and a tool an agent calls, as an architect's cell will, is one call with one result, which a route is and a stream is not.
