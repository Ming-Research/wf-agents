# Failed rounds

### decision a-shared-cause-is-one-wait
A round that fails at the account's usage limit, for a command line that cannot run, or for want of the network holds the agents' rounds of every work, as one wait worked out from the records of every work; no work is paused for it, and every work goes on by itself once the wait is over.
Why: One cause stops every work alike: paused one by one, each would wait for the owner to start it by hand once the cause had passed, and a mark written on each would have to be lifted on each.

### decision the-shared-wait-holds-only-agents
The shared wait holds only the rounds of agents; the engine's own acts, and the making of calls and taking of what came back, go on.
Why: The causes it waits out are the agents' service's, and holding an act that needs no agent, as the old engine held every step, waits for nothing.

### decision no-round-tries-the-network
While the network cannot be reached, the engine looks at it itself, which costs no round, and a round tries only once a look reaches it, or now and then in case what is looked at is not the service; a look's finding is not recorded, and after a stop the engine looks again.
Why: The command line writes a round's message into its session before it calls the service, and retrying rounds through an outage filled a session with the same input again and again.

### decision the-retry-wait-is-a-call
The wait before a round that failed in passing is tried again is a call of the run that the engine makes and the clock answers at its time, its end counted from the failed round's end.
Why: Deciding then needs no clock, a wait made is known as made after a stop, and the work shows what it waits for and until when as for any call; deciding with the time as an input was turned down, since a decision could then not be taken again from the records alone.

### decision unknown-failures-pass
A failure of the agents' command line that no kind's words tell apart is taken as passing and tried again, while a round that could not be carried out before its program ran pauses the work.
Why: The service's passing errors are many and change their wording, and taking an unknown one as passing costs at most a few tries before the bound pauses the work; a fault of the engine's own before the program runs comes back however often it is tried.

### decision an-unread-return-is-sent-back
A return that could not be read is sent back to the agent in its session, as any return that does not meet the form, and counts against the run's limit of returns sent back.
Why: The agent's work is done and only its hand-in is wrong, so running the round from its start would pay for it twice; repairing the text in the engine or by a cheap model was turned down, since the command line's one result does not give the text of the attempts it refused.

### decision a-flagged-round-begins-a-new-session
A round the service's safety check flagged is given again at once in a new session of its line, whose name is worked out from how many of the line's rounds were flagged; flagged again, with only failures between, the work is paused.
Why: The same session would give the check the same context again, more likely the cause than the task's words, though that cannot be shown; a second flag says what the agent is given needs a look.

### decision a-note-run-is-held-only-by-its-own-failure
While a work is paused, a run that answers a note has its round after a failure held only where that failure paused the work.
Why: A failure that is tried again by itself, or waits with every work, is no cause the owner must see first, and holding it would leave the note unanswered for as long as the owner's own pause lasts.
