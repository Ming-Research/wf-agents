- Each matter the architect handles is a one-line bubble in the one-to-one: moving while in hand, updated to a line of
  result when done, and left in the history, so that it looks like the architect reporting as it works. The owner sees
  its current state, and discussing the policy comes naturally.

- A matter's result has one form, on the same line as its state, which takes no line of its own; that would be a waste.

- When the architect creates items or records observations, one post lists each title, which jumps there when clicked,
  with no card for each observation. This is important news, and the same batch of observations shown in two places
  would repeat itself.

- The owner's words get no matter card; their state is a line of small text below the bubble. One sentence as bubble and
  card would be there twice.

- The policy tab shows the policy in force, with a draft on top while it awaits approval; research and check records are
  not there. It is there for the owner to recall what policy the work goes by, approved when it was set, and research is
  not policy, though placed there it would look like it.

- The architect's notebook can be turned back to earlier versions, showing what each time changed. What the architect
  keeps for itself, if the owner cannot see it, is unsettling.

- The queue is in the window to the right of the one-to-one, showing how much is left and how much was handled, not a
  count of wakes. At the conversation's top right its place and use were odd, and the owner cares about the queue and
  what is handled.

- Holding new works is a button on the page, which the assistant and the architect can also use, with its notice at the
  right of the status line, apart in look from the current node. That line has much empty room at its right. Setting the
  parallel count to zero to stop slowly was rejected: waiting works would get no slot and could not go on.

- Holding new works asks why in a row under the title row, not in the dialog other commands that reach past what they
  name ask in (frontend/design-system.md, "A command whose effect reaches past what it names"): the owner decides by
  that row and the architect's state beside it, which a dialog would hide.
