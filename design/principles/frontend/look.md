# The look of the owner's page

How everything on the owner's page looks. The values behind these rules (the colours, the sizes, the heights) and the
parts they are built into are kept with the page's code.

### principle dense-page
Spacing follows one small grid, and the bar, a view's line, a row and a control each have one fixed, small height.
Why: The owner reads many works and many blocks at once, and every row spent on chrome is a row of content lost
(principle room-goes-to-content).

### principle full-width
Content takes the full width of the window: no centred column, and no cards round what is already in a table.
Why: A chart and a table need every column (principle room-goes-to-content).

### principle few-sizes-two-weights
Text has four sizes and two weights, and no others: one size for labels and tags, one for secondary words and everything
in monospace, one for body text, and one, semibold, for the title of the page and of a panel. Numbers are tabular.
Why: A few sizes far enough apart read as an order at a glance.

### principle monospace-for-names-and-values
Ids, the names of blocks, values and versions are in monospace.
Why: They are what the owner types and compares letter by letter.

### principle colour-only-for-state
A colour marks a state or a mark, never decoration. A mark or a tag takes the colour itself, small text its darker
step, and a fill its tint.
Why: So that a colour always means one thing (principle scarce-emphasis).

### principle hairlines-no-shadows
Parts are divided by hairlines in one line colour, and nothing casts a shadow. Tags, buttons and inputs have slightly
rounded corners; panels and frames have none.
Why: Depth is not information.

### principle quiet-motion
Hovering changes a background by one step, quickly, and nothing else on the page moves.
Why: So that what changes on the page is the owner's doing or a real change (principle owner-keeps-their-place).

### principle focus-always-shows
Whatever holds the keyboard's focus shows it by an outline in the accent.
Why: So that the page can be used with the keyboard.

### principle widths-are-the-owners
An edge that divides two parts drags, shows under the pointer that it does, keeps its width in the browser, and goes
back to the width the page works out on a double click.
Why: Cut words can then be read in full where they stand.

### principle say-a-thing-once
What belongs to the whole view is said in the view's line, and a panel says only what differs from it.
Why: Each thing has one place and is shown there once (Article 8).
