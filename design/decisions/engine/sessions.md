# Sessions

When a line of a work goes on in its session of the agents' program and when it begins a new one.

### decision a-new-session-loads-again-through-the-block
A new session of a line that has a cell reading a block the outside source `session` writes writes that block, so that the cell runs again before the line goes on; a new session of a line with no such cell only begins. Each line has a block of its own, `<line>_session`, made by a trigger every line has its own of, and a new session of one line writes its block alone.
Why: That cell's whole job is to read into the session what the line's later cells work from, and a new session holds nothing of it; written as the outside, the block makes the cell run by the one rule that runs any cell, and its run changing nothing it wrote, nothing after it runs again. A line whose cells each read what they need from their message loses nothing a block would bring back. One block for every line ran the loading cell of each other line again for nothing, a round of the deep research at its highest effort among them, at every new session or compaction of the engineer's line; a block of each cell's own was turned down, since a session is a line's and the cells of a line share it.

### decision a-lost-session-keeps-its-id
A session the agents' program no longer has is begun again under the same id, and a compacted one goes on under it; only a new session for another reason takes an id of its own.
Why: Which session a round runs in is counted from the new sessions recorded before its first try began, and the round that finds its session lost began its first try before that is recorded: under an id of its own, the session that round begins would be another than the one the rounds after it count.

### decision no-new-session-when-the-cache-expired
A line does not begin a new session because its prompt cache expired while it waited: a round after the cache's lifetime goes on in the session, writing it to the cache once.
Why: Going on writes the session to the cache once, while a new session runs the loading cell again and reads again what its rounds had read, which costs no less (judgment, not measured). Offered as a choice of each line, it was taken by none.

### decision waiting-sessions-are-kept-warm
While a work that has not ended waits, with no round of it under way, the session of each of its lines it can come back to (a line of a cell where the work stands, or one the data its cells write reach) is sent a keep-warm ping `sessions.warm_minutes` after its last call, while the cache keeps it, as many times in one wait as its model's prices make worth it; one that failed is tried again a minute after. A ping runs with the command line of the session's last round and takes no slot; it waits for the wait every work shares, for the owner after the engine held everything as it opened, and, as `sessions.warm_while_paused` says, while the owner holds everything; it counts in the work's spend, wherever the page shows what the work or the span cost. Nothing else of the work runs beside its pings: no round of it begins until they have ended, and what else is to be done for it, as letting go of its worktree, waits for them.
Why: A round after the cache's life writes the whole session again: a write kept an hour costs forty reads on Opus and Sonnet 5.5, twenty on Haiku 5.5 and eighty on Fable 5.1, and a fork that missed its base wrote 73,000 of its 107,000 tokens again for $0.57 more where a ping cost $0.03. Pings in a wait are bounded by what one write would cost beyond a read, 39 on Opus and Sonnet, 19 on Haiku, 79 on Fable; a line the work has passed for good would be pinged for nothing. A round begun beside a ping would be filled as its session stood before the ping, which may compact it, and its cost counted without the ping's; a worktree let go of under a ping takes the directory its program runs in.

### decision a-session-is-replaced-past-its-limit
A line begins a new session where its last round ended with more context than its limit: the line the rounds of the line's other sessions ended with give, in every work of the flow (decision `a-line-comes-from-the-history-of-its-kind`), but never more than `sessions.context_limit`, which is the limit while that history is too short to give a line. A line whose round failed for a prompt too long for the model begins a new session, which the round is given again in once the work is let on.
Why: A line from history replaces a session that grew past what its line's sessions explain sooner than a fixed number would, and a session's own earlier rounds would raise its line as it grows and never let it be cut. But the program compacts a session near its model's window, so once one session of a line came near the window, the line stands past it and cuts nothing again; and until the history has its values there is no line at all. A fixed number is kept under both, since only it still bounds what each call of a long session reads. A session that holds a prompt too long would be too long for every round after it.

### decision the-projects-files-from-the-worktree
A session's system prompt is made as the session begins, with the project's files (its guide, its constitution, its tree's rules) read from the work's worktree by the paths the declaration of the work's project, `flow/project.toml` on its main, names, never through a symbolic link, and kept with the session in the data directory; every round of the session, however it begins in it, is given the kept one. The engine does not start where main, as the host last gave it, lacks one of the files or holds one through a link, and a round whose worktree lacks one fails.
Why: The owner ruled that a change of these files made in a work counts in that work: they are the owner's to change (principle protected-files-are-the-owners), and a change of them on a work's branch is meant to hold there. What keeps a work from changing the rules that govern its own agents is that the files are protected, not where the prompt is read from; read from main instead, a change the owner made on the branch would not reach the work. A link is refused because it would carry into the prompt a file the protection does not cover. The prompt is kept, since the worktree changes within a session, by the work's own commits and by main taken in, and a session keeps its system prompt as it began (principle the-system-prompt-holds-what-a-line-shares). A file missing is said rather than left out, since a session without its project's rules would look like one with them (principle nothing-passes-for-normal).

### decision lines-say-where-sessions-begin
No cell begins a new session of its line at a place of its own: a cell that must not go on in another's session is on a line of its own.
Why: Lines say which cells share a session; a place within a line where its session began anew was turned down as a second way of saying the same thing, with a mark of its own on the chart.

### decision a-compaction-reopens-the-session-under-its-id
A compaction of a line's session by the agents' program during a round is recorded as the session reopened, once for each compaction, while the round is under way, and the session goes on under its id.
Why: A compacted session holds a summary where it held what the line read, so the cell that reads into it must run again, and the rule that runs any cell, a new version of its input (document data-and-context, 25), does that only if the compaction is a new version of the data `session`. The program keeps the session under its id, so a new id would have the next round begin a session that exists (decision a-lost-session-keeps-its-id).
