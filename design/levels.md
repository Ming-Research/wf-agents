### decision a-level-is-a-difficulty
An item has one level, which says only how hard its work is and chooses the model its agents run on; how hard each step thinks, its reasoning effort, is set by the step in the flow, the same for every item. A level's description speaks of difficulty alone.
Why: Levels that bound a model and an effort together were tried and turned down: a step that only gathers needs less thought than one that designs whatever the item, and an item's difficulty has nothing to do with that. Describing levels by whether decisions are needed misled whoever gave an item its level, since that is not difficulty.

### decision effort-is-the-flows
A step's reasoning effort is part of the flow's definition and changes only by changing the flow, which the owner approves on the comparison; there is no setting of it on the page or in the installation. A step of searching or judging is not given the lowest effort, even on the cheapest model.
Why: An effort is a property of what the step does, which the flow says, and a setting beside the flow would let the two drift unseen. The cheapest model at the lowest effort judged too poorly.

### decision only-deep-research-runs-above
The deep research runs one level above its item's; every other line, the reviews among them, runs at the item's own. No line runs above the highest level an item may be at, so that an item's work never reaches the top level when the installation keeps that out of items.
Why: A weaker agent doing the deep research may fix the surface and stop, hiding a larger problem that no later step finds, and few works take that way, so the higher model costs little in all. A review is a second pair of eyes after which the implementation is judged again, and it runs on far more works. The top level costs out of proportion to its gain for everyday work, which is why an installation may hold items below it.

### decision raised-by-the-line-not-by-name
How far above its item's level a line runs is said by the line in the flow's definition. The engine and the installation name no line.
Why: The engine before named its lines in code to raise them, so a line renamed or added was raised or not without anyone seeing, and the flow the owner reads did not show it.

### decision what-a-round-ran-on-is-kept-with-it
What each try of an agent's round ran on, its level, that level's model and its effort, is kept beside what the try was told, as the try begins, and the past is read from that and never worked out again from the item's level and the settings. A round given again after the engine started anew is run on the settings then, and keeps what it ran on with that try.
Why: The settings may give a level another model and the flow may raise a line or change an effort, and a round read by today's would say it ran on what it did not. A record of the run in the store was turned down: what a round ran on is chosen by the one who runs it, as the message it is told is, and is kept where that message is.

### decision an-item-of-no-level
An item kept from before items had levels has none, is shown so, and its rounds run at the default level. An item whose level the settings no longer have, or now hold above the highest, is not run at another level: its round fails, saying why, and the work waits for the owner.
Why: No level could be given to those items without guessing; they are few and end as they are. Running an item quietly at a level other than its own would spend at a model nobody chose for it.
