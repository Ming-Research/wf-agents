Decision: The engine's loop is the only scheduler of the works: between steps it alone reads what the owner asked, carries out the outbox, brings works to the flow version it runs, starts works and grants slots, and it carries out the engine's own actions on a work from outside its step (a start over asked, the takeover of a work on an older flow version) only while that work has no job in flight; it hands each step it dispatches to a thread of its own with an execution context of its own, never two jobs of one work at once, so that the rounds and tool runs of different works overlap while each work still moves one step after another and the owner's pause of a work still takes effect when its round under way ends; instead of the loop taking one step of one work at a time, because a step waits on an agent's command line or on a CI run for minutes, and while it waited every other work stood with its slot taken and unused (observed in the owner's database: no two works' agent rounds or CI runs ever overlapped while several works were alive together); and instead of a step carrying out the outbox, or another work's start over, itself, because the outbox performs each action once only while one place carries it out, and a step that started another work over would cut off that work's round while its own step still ran.

Decision: Only the loop grants slots: a work that holds no slot, because it waits, takes one for a step only when the loop grants it a free slot as it dispatches that step, a granted step counting as holding the slot until it ends, and the step's own check whether the work may leave its wait reads that grant, instead of each step counting the free slots itself, because two steps counting at once would both see the same free slot and go on beside the works that hold the others, or, each counting the other as holding one, neither would go on.

Decision: A ping that keeps a session warm is dispatched by the loop as its work's one job in flight, on a thread of its own as a step is, and the session manager still refuses a ping beside an open round of the session, because a ping is a call of the agent's command line that would hold up every dispatch while it ran on the loop's own thread, and a ping and a round of one session at once would interleave two turns in one conversation.

Decision: Ending a round ends it only while it is open: when no open round matches, the write that ends it fails and the step's transaction is rolled back, and a step whose work ended meanwhile takes nothing of what its round handed in, because a work may be ended from outside its step at any time, by the owner cancelling its item on the page or by an architect's change cancelling it, which cuts its round off, and a step whose round was so ended would otherwise go on to move its work as if it had ended the round itself, writing over what ended it; instead of every ending of a work waiting until its step ends, because the owner's cancelling takes effect at once and a round that goes on after its item was cancelled spends for nothing; and instead of a version of each work checked by every write, because the owner's pause of a work also writes the work and would roll back the step it should let finish, while under one job of a work at a time the only change that conflicts with a step is its round being ended.

Decision: While a failed run's cause stops every work, a cause another work's round meets meanwhile leaves standing whichever of the two waits ends later, and the loop's look at the network ends or prolongs only the wait it looked at, so that no step is dispatched before every cause known to have stopped the works has passed; instead of each new cause's wait replacing the one that stood, because with the rounds of several works side by side two may fail together, and a wait of half a minute for the network set over a usage limit that resets in an hour would let rounds run before the limit reset; and instead of one wait kept for each cause at once, because its one gain, knowing without a round that the network is still down once a longer wait ends, costs a round's failed run while a change of how the engine and the owner's page tell why every work stopped would cost every reader of it.

Rejected:
- A thread for each work that loops over its own steps: rejected because pausing, the halt, the owner's Step, the slots and the outbox would then be decided in every work's thread instead of in one place between steps.
- A fixed pool of threads fed from a queue: rejected because a step takes minutes, so reusing threads saves nothing, while the pool's size would be a second quota beside the slots, the architects, the pings and the owner's Step, and a job waiting in the queue would again hold up another.
- Each step's thread carrying out its own actions of the outbox and taking slots by reservations in the database: rejected because actions claimed by work would need a way to finish those a crash left claimed, which one place carrying out the outbox does not need, and reservations kept in the database would outlive the process they mean anything in, while the loop starting works would still read them.
- Splitting each step into preparing, waiting and finishing, with only the waits moved off the loop's thread: rejected because every step that waits (an agent's round, each CI tool, the merge, the watch of main) would have to be suspended and resumed with its state kept between the parts, while a step on a thread of its own stays one straight function.
- An asynchronous engine on an async runtime: rejected because every function of the engine, the session manager and the GitHub client would turn asynchronous while the database library stays synchronous, for no more concurrent steps than the machines have slots.
- A lock of each work held by its step until the step ends, which cancelling the work's item takes first: rejected because the owner's page and an architect's step would wait on a round of minutes, one thing holding up another again, and an architect cancelling several items would take several locks in turn.
- Ranking the causes that stop every work, a usage limit above an expired login above no network, the higher kept: rejected because a cause ranked higher whose wait ends sooner would still let steps start before the wait it replaced ended, which the time each wait ends decides and a rank does not.
