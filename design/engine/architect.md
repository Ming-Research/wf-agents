Decision: Each project has an architect of its own, which judges only that project: a wake is a work of the flow's architect graph for one project (`works.project`), on no item, holding no slot, and each project has its own queue, holds on new starts, session line, state of things, notes, log, policy and observations; one wake of a project runs at a time, and wakes of several projects may run together; architects message one another as matters of kind `other_architect` in the receiver's queue, instead of one architect over every project, because each project's intent is its own constitution and policy, and an architect that holds one project in its context judges it better than one that holds them all.

Decision: A wake goes from W0 (what woke it) to W1, which sends a session that has not understood the project since it started or was last compacted through U1 (what the project is now), U2 (does the policy in force still govern it) and U3 (does what runs fit), and one that has straight to the queue at D0, instead of answering the first questions at every wake, because the understanding a session holds is what lets it judge, and asking for it again in a session that holds it costs the loop's context and adds nothing.

Decision: When the architect's line would start a new session in the middle of a wake (the owner asked for one, the last was past the limit, the model changed) at a cell past the understanding and outside the setting of a policy, the wake goes back to W1, which sends it to U1, and a matter picked but not handled goes back to the queue, instead of the new session taking that cell, because a new session holds no state of things and no notes: one started at a matter's path moved an item against the owner's earlier request, which only the architect's notes held.

Decision: What the architect handles is a matter in its project's queue (`architect_matters`), of one of eight kinds, each with its path: `set_straight` (T1), `engineer_question` (Q1 then Q2), `report` (I1 then I2), `design_card` (K1), `failure` (F1), `owner_words` (O1), `other_architect` (M1) and `slots_free` (N1), with a `detail` that says more (an engineer's question's own kind, a side report's kind, the owner's words' kind); D0 takes the lone matter at once, or D1 picks one among several by the constitution and the policy, and an engine router sends it down its path, instead of one cell for every matter, because each kind needs other material and other judgment, and a path cell given only its matter's material keeps the loop's context small.

Decision: The queue and the batch are standard-context inputs, so that a session is given each once and after that only what changed in it, each time with the numbers of the matters that can be picked and of those the batch handled, so that it loses track of none; a path cell reads its matter's material, the items it concerns and nothing broad; the broad inputs (the board, the numbers, the overview of observations, the policy, the constitution) go to U1, U2 and U3, the numbers measured up to the wake's start so that they hold through it; and a question's second cell (Q2, I2) refers to its first cell's answer, as the cells that judge and correct a policy being set (P6, P7, P11) refer to the return that wrote it, instead of being given it again, because the owner requires the doing loop's context to stay small.

Decision: An engineer's question stops the work at cell 15, holding no slot, until the architect's reply, which the engine takes back unchanged; a reply that waits for item changes of its round is held until the review of their batch lets them be made, and then goes with the numbers of the items made, instead of the owner answering in the architect's place, because the architect runs, and an engineer must not go on from an item that is not there yet.

Decision: A path cell hands in, with its answer, effects of one shape: item changes, which are checked as they come (`arch_changes::validate`, a failure going back to the cell) and collected in the wake's batch, and which may name an item an earlier change of the batch creates by its key, the engine giving it its number as it makes the batch in order; observation changes, actions (pause or resume a work, hold or release new starts, interject), messages to other architects and notes, which apply at once and are logged; after each matter B1 goes back to the queue until no matter can be taken or twenty were handled, and the batch's item changes, if it collected any, go to R1, a review on its own line in a new session, whose findings X1 revises, three looks at most, after which what is still wrong is dropped and put to the owner in one ask holding the matters it came from; C1 then rethinks the whole after the batch, instead of the owner approving every change, because the owner wants the changes made by a reviewed judgment and reads them in the log, and only what the review cannot settle needs the owner.

Decision: An ask of the owner from a path cell holds only its own matter, recording the cell that asked (`resume_cell`): the path ends for now and the wake goes on with the other matters; the owner's answer puts the matter back in the queue, and when it is taken again the router sends it to the cell that asked, with the answer, instead of the wake waiting for the owner, because the owner may take hours to answer and the other matters need not wait for one.

Decision: Observations are a record beside the items: something worth doing that no item does yet, with its tags from the project's fixed set (`observation_tags` in the project's file), where it came from, how many times it was reported and what it refers to; S1, on its own line `observer` at the tier `[agents] observer` names (`1` by default), searches the open ones for the cell that asks with `search`, and an item that takes one in closes it once its work merges, instead of findings left in notes, because what is seen and not yet worth an item must neither be lost nor become an item before its time.

Decision: The architect's session goes on from one wake of its project to the next, and the engine counts what it holds from the rounds of every wake that used it; the first wake, the first after a new policy took effect, one past the session limit, one the owner asks for and one on another model start a new session; R1 starts one for each batch, which its reviews after revisions continue, S1 one at the first search of each wake, and the new architect that reads a policy is on no line, instead of a new session each wake, because the architect's understanding of its project is what lets it judge, and a review or a reader that knew anything but what it checks would not check it.

Decision: New starts of a project stop while its architect holds them (an action), while U3 found what runs unfit and T1 sets it straight, and while a new policy is set (U2's set again, through P1 to P11), each a row of `dispatch_holds` for the project, and works already running go on, instead of a single switch, because each hold ends on its own: the architect releases its own, setting straight ends when its matter is handled, and a new policy ends when it takes effect or the owner says it stays.

Decision: The dispatch order is the architect's: each item has a rank among its project's items, which the architect's item changes set (reorder), and the engine starts ready items by rank, taking projects in turn, instead of a plan, because the architect judges what comes first by the constitution and the policy.

Decision: While a wake's batch is open, the engine starts no work on an item a change the batch collected names (the item it changes, the item a split splits or another is moved under, the items a merge keeps and absorbs), and once the changes are made or dropped the items are as `ready_items` says, instead of starting items as they stand meanwhile, because a change such as a new dependency is made only after the review of the whole batch, and a work started before it goes against what the architect decided.

Decision: The architect does not fix what it is asked about itself: a tool that failed or main's environment that changed is fixed by an item, with the check added to the known red checks meanwhile where main is red, or by the owner for what only the owner can do, and the architect replies once it is fixed, instead of the architect fixing it in its own wake, because the architect reads and judges its project and changes its code nowhere, and a fix is an engineer's work, reviewed and tested as any other.

Decision: A design card the owner has not confirmed is a matter of the architect's, which does not rule on it: K1 looks at whether the card is sound and its defaults reasonable in the whole, and acts when they are not, such as interjecting, and otherwise leaves the card to the owner, whose confirmation of every decision alone lets the work merge; a design card matter whose card was confirmed or replaced before it was picked is closed by the engine as it looks at the queue, saying why, instead of the architect ruling beside the owner, because the owner rules on design cards.

Decision: A work that delivered a report queues, as it ends, the latest what to do next it handed in, as a `report` matter with the report's paths and the commit merged; a side report is a `report` matter of its own, one marked urgent also reaching a running wake at the top of its next round, instead of reports collected for later, because waking the architect when it is needed costs no more, and an urgent report must reach a running architect at once.

Decision: The engine queues `slots_free` when fewer of the project's items are ready to start than there are free slots, at most once an hour while such a matter waits or was handled; provisional: the hour keeps an empty queue from waking the architect again and again until how long slots stand empty has been measured.

Decision: Why the architect pauses a work or holds new starts is written in the owner's language, kept with the pause or the hold, and shown on the page; a work it paused is let go on by its resume or by the owner's Start of that work, which is told to the architect as the owner's words, instead of a third kind of pause, because what matters to the owner is who stopped the work and that it stays stopped.

Decision: The architect answers each of the owner's words in the owner's messages (`tell_owner`), saying what it made of them and what it did: at O1 for the owner's words that come as a matter, and in the round that took them for words the owner said to a wake while a round ran, instead of the owner reading what it made of them only in its log, because the owner talks with the architect in a window of its own on the page and the conversation goes on only when every word is answered.

Decision: E1 wraps a wake up, writing what it learned into the state of things, and E2 ends it, the session staying for the next wake, instead of a wake that ends when its last matter is handled, because what a wake learned is what the next wake's understanding builds on.

Rejected:
- One architect over every project: rejected because each project has its own intent and the context of all of them is too large to judge one.
- The owner approving each batch of item changes: rejected; a review in a new session checks them, and the owner reads them in the log.
- The architect ruling on design cards beside the owner: rejected because the owner rules on design cards; the architect only looks at whether a card and its defaults hold.
- A wake per event: rejected because each would understand the project again for one matter.
