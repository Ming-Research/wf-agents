### principle owner-taste-judges
A design on the owner's page is judged by the owner's taste and use; user research and general design guidelines are no argument against them.
Why: wf-agents serves only its owner; research and guidelines are the grounds of products made for the public. (Article 8)

### principle scarce-emphasis
The strongest marks (a filled background, colour, motion) and the first places go to what matters most, weaker marks and later places to the rest; one meaning has one colour.
Why: Strong marks spent on small things leave nothing to make the important stand out; when everything stands out, nothing does.

### principle one-primary-command
A view has one primary command, the one it exists for. A command whose effect reaches beyond what its name says asks before it acts, saying what will happen.
Why: The primary button tells the owner what the view is for; the owner must know the consequences before they happen.

### principle room-goes-to-content
Room on the screen goes to content: a control or fact shares a row rather than taking one, and at every width the page fits the window, the least needed part giving way first.
Why: The chart and the lists need the height and the width; each extra row was taken from what it shows, a page that scrolled sideways hid its edge, and cells squeezed to one letter a line could not be read. (Article 8)

### principle owner-keeps-their-place
What the owner reads and acts on stays put: a control stands with what it governs, and nothing in view moves or changes unless the owner did it or the thing shown really changed.
Why: A control pressed from a view about something else did what the owner could not see; a list should not move under the owner's hand, and one that changes under the eyes cannot be read. (Article 8)
