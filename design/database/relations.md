Decision: Things in the database relate in three ways only: owning (B is a part of A, belongs to exactly one A and never moves), pointing (A uses B, B exists on its own and many A may point to it) and depending (only between items: a pointing that means A cannot be finished until B is), instead of relations left implicit in the columns, because whether a row can be deleted, moved or shared follows from which of the three it is in.

Decision: An item points to its project and depends on other items; an item owns its works, at most one running at a time; a work points to its flow version, machine and pull request, owns its lines, each line owns its sessions, each session its keep-warm pings, and the work owns its rounds and interjections; a round points to its cell and session and owns its tool runs, measurements, side reports, design cards, asks of the owner and messages to the owner; an interjection points to the round it was delivered in; the dispatch queue points to items; a known red check points to its project and to the item that fixes it; the change records and the outbox point to any row; works only point to each other, because each owned thing then has one place it belongs and is deleted with it.

Decision: A design card belongs to the round that handed it in, and an ask of the owner to the round that put it, not to the item, because the round holds what was decided or asked when it was written, and the item's later rounds can replace a design card.
