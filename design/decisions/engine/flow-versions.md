# The versions of the flows

Which version of its flow a work runs on, what is kept of a version, and how what a work recorded on an earlier one is
read.

### decision a-version-is-kept-as-its-files-and-its-outline
A version of a flow is kept once, named by the digest of its files: each file once by its own digest, and its outline,
what reading a work on it needs of it (each cell in order with its id, title and line, whether an agent runs it, what
it is given, watches and reads, and the datum it speaks with the owner in; the lines, each with the digest of what the
version decides of its form, the blocks its cells write, each in its shape, and the tools the flow names; the groups;
what the card shows; the table of earlier ids; how a datum is named for a cell), made from the compiled flow as the
engine starts with the version. A work on a version not installed is read from its records and that outline alone,
and no version is compiled again. An outline's parts are only added, each one that versions kept before may lack, and
none is renamed or retyped.
Why: Compiled again at each start, every version a work ended on would bind each later change of the flow's layout or
of the compiler's checks to keep reading it, and the compiler would carry every old reading for good: the flows W1 to
W11 ran on no longer compile, their keys refused since #335 and #341. The parts of the outline have stood in every flow
since the first. Keeping the compiled flow, or the page's whole view of it, was turned down: each changes with the code.
The files are kept for the past as it ran (principle the-past-as-it-ran), for whoever reads them with `query`; each
once, since a version is about 61 KB and main changed the flow 51 times in eight days, most files unchanged each time
(principle stored-content-is-kept-small).

### decision a-move-is-read-through-the-renumberings
A work's records keep every name as recorded; a work put on another version records the version alone, and what it
recorded before is read through the renumberings of the table of earlier ids between the version before and the one
after, as their outlines have them, each move in turn. What passed between the engine and an agent's session, what a
round was given, what came back and what was filled into the session, is read as recorded, and what a session holds
as decision a-datum-a-move-renamed-is-given-again-whole says. The version a work is added on is its first entry.
Why: What a move renames is worked out from the outlines kept, so it is not stored beside them (principle
state-from-records). Writing the new names over the records was turned down (decision tables-only-added-to). What a
session was told, it was told under the names it then had (principle the-past-as-it-ran).

### decision a-work-that-ended-stays-on-its-version
A work that ended stays on the version it ended on, and is decided, let go of and drawn by it; at each start every
other work is put on the version installed, and a work reopened is put on it as it is reopened. So a work on a version
not installed has ended, as the walk reads an end: the outline's last cell has its record, or its cancel stands. A
round of such a work is judged in the history of its cell as the cell it is on the version installed, and only in the
flow's history where its cell has no id there.
Why: Read on the version installed, a work that ended is read by a flow it never ran on (principle the-past-as-it-ran):
a cell added after its last would make a merged work go on. A work going on runs on the one flow there is (principle
one-flow). A cell's history is its rounds on every version under the id it has now (principle judged-by-own-history);
a cell taken out is no cell's.

### decision a-table-that-misreads-a-kept-version-is-refused
The engine does not start while the installed flow's table of earlier ids does not begin with the whole table of a
version a work is on, reads two cells of such a version as one, or reads one of them as a cell the installed flow
neither has nor took out; it says which version, which cells and which works.
Why: What those works recorded could not then be read as the cells and data it ran at (principle
nothing-passes-for-normal), and the table is the one thing of a kept version that a later flow must keep. The compiler
sees only the installed flow and its table, so it cannot tell an id given in a stage the renumbering moves no cell of
from a free one, as `D3` given `P1` where a `P1` stood, nor a cell gone without being taken out; the outlines kept can,
for every version a work is on. Holding such works at fault and starting all the same was turned down: the fault is
the flow's, and is mended there.

### decision a-work-kept-before-the-versions-is-on-none
A work kept before the versions were is put, at the first start that finds it, on no version where it has ended as the
flow installed then reads it, and on that version otherwise. A work on no version has ended, has no outline, is decided
by no flow and counts only in its flow's history; reopened, it is put on the version installed with its records read as
recorded, the nearest reading there is.
Why: Nothing kept says which version such a work ran on, and the versions W1 to W11 ran on no longer compile (the
research of W21); put on the version installed, it was read by a flow it never ran on, the fault this guards against.
It ended under the reading every earlier engine gave it, and that reading is taken once.

### decision a-work-keeps-what-its-time-went-to
As a work ends, while the version it ended on is installed, the engine keeps what its time went to: each stretch from
its first round to its last, run by a model or by the tools, or waited for the owner, for a pause, for the items it
depends on or for nothing the bar names, as the one definition of what waits on the owner reads it then. A work that
ended is drawn from what it kept; one that kept nothing has its rounds read from its records and its waits not known.
Why: What a work waited for at a moment is the walk's and that definition's reading on its whole flow (principle
one-count-of-what-waits), which a work on a version no longer installed is not read by; read from the records alone,
its waits on the owner at a limit, at a precondition or stalled would be lost, and kept nowhere, they would change on
the page when another flow was installed (principle owner-keeps-their-place). Principle state-from-records keeps from
beside the records what can be worked out from them; this can be worked out only on the version compiled, and once
that version is not installed nothing works it out again (decision a-version-is-kept-as-its-files-and-its-outline), so
it is kept as the work ends, while it still can be.
