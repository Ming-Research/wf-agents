# Sessions

When a line of a work goes on in its session of the agents' program and when it begins a new one.

### decision a-new-session-loads-again-through-its-line-s-datum
A new session of a line writes the line's own `session` datum, `<line>_session`, which the flow has only where a cell of the line watches it, so that the cell runs again before the line goes on; a new session of a line with no such cell only begins. Of the engineer flow only the engineer's line has one: the deep research watches none, its first round reading the tree as its task says (document data-and-context, 25).
Why: That cell's whole job is to read into the session what the line's later cells work from, and a new session holds nothing of it; written as the outside, the datum makes the cell run by the one rule that runs any cell, and its run changing nothing it wrote, nothing after it runs again. A line whose cells each read what they need from their message loses nothing a datum would bring back. One datum for every line ran the loading cell of each other line again for nothing, a round of the deep research at its highest effort among them, at every new session or compaction of the engineer's line; on the deep research, watching its own line's datum, it ran its whole round again for a new session where its first round reads the tree anyway; a datum of each cell's own was turned down, since a session is a line's and the cells of a line share it.

### decision a-lost-session-keeps-its-id
A session the agents' program no longer has is begun again under the same id, and a compacted one goes on under it; only a new session for another reason takes an id of its own.
Why: Which session a round runs in is counted from the new sessions recorded before its first try began, and the round that finds its session lost began its first try before that is recorded: under an id of its own, the session that round begins would be another than the one the rounds after it count.

### decision a-line-goes-on-in-its-session-under-another-account
A line goes on in its session after the owner takes another account: no session begins anew for an account, and the line's next round writes the session to the new account's cache, its miss told with the account as what differs before the session's messages.
Why: The session lives in the one directory of the program's, and the service takes a session made under another account: a session made under one subscription account, holding a signed thinking block, was copied into another account's directory and resumed, and the model answered from it (`claude` 2.1.327, Sonnet 5.5). A new session for the account was turned down: it would hold nothing of what the line had read and done, and run the line's loading cell again (principle keep-what-was-paid-for), where going on costs one write of the cache.

### decision no-new-session-when-the-cache-expired
A line does not begin a new session because its prompt cache expired while it waited: a round after the cache's lifetime goes on in the session, writing it to the cache once.
Why: Going on writes the session to the cache once, while a new session runs the loading cell again and reads again what its rounds had read, which costs no less (judgment, not measured). Offered as a choice of each line, it was taken by none.

### decision waiting-sessions-are-kept-warm
While a work that has not ended waits, with no round of it under way, the session of each of its lines it can come back to (a line of a cell where the work stands, or one the data its cells write reach) is sent a keep-warm ping `sessions.warm_minutes` after its last call, while the cache keeps it, as many times in one wait as its model's prices make worth it; one that failed is tried again a minute after. A ping runs with the command line of the session's last round and takes no slot, and it counts in the work's spend, wherever the page shows what the work or the span cost. It is sent whoever holds the work: the engine as it opened, the owner's pause of everything or of the work alone, or a question the work waits on the owner for. Two holds stop it: the wait every work shares, which it waits for as a round does, and the engine's pause of a work for spending more than it may, under which none is sent. Whether the sessions of works that wait are kept warm at all is the engine's own state, kept with the installation as the pause of everything is and switched on the owner's page, and no setting: they are kept warm until the owner switches it off. Nothing else of the work runs beside its pings: no round of it begins until they have ended, and what else is to be done for it, as letting go of its worktree, waits for them. A ping runs under the account in use, and a session whose last call ran under another account is not pinged.
Why: A round after the cache's life writes the whole session again: a write kept an hour costs forty reads on Opus and Sonnet 5.5, twenty on Haiku 5.5 and eighty on Fable 5.1, and a fork that missed its base wrote 73,000 of its 107,000 tokens again for $0.57 more where a ping cost $0.03. Pings in a wait are bounded by what one write would cost beyond a read, 39 on Opus and Sonnet, 19 on Haiku, 79 on Fable; a line the work has passed for good would be pinged for nothing. A hold is on the work going on and not on letting go of what the work has already paid for (principle keep-what-was-paid-for): with each hold stopping the pings of the works it held, no ping ever ran on the installation, and a work waited eight hours at a cell with its session let go cold. A ping under the engine's pause for the budget would spend where the budget is the refusal to let that work spend more, and one under the wait every work shares would fail as the rounds that wait for it do. A setting read as the engine starts would leave a switch over it doing nothing until the engine started again, which is worse than no switch; kept as a setting as well, one thing would stand in two places (principle one-thing-one-place). A round begun beside a ping would be filled as its session stood before the ping, which may compact it, and its cost counted without the ping's; a worktree let go of under a ping takes the directory its program runs in. A cache is one account's: a ping under the account in use reads nothing a session's calls under another wrote, and would write the session whole, as its next round does anyway.

### decision a-session-is-compacted-at-the-installations-number
Every round's command line gives the agents' program `sessions.compact_at`, the installation's own number, as the context at which it compacts the line's session, and the engine begins no new session for a session's size; no line drawn from the history of the line's rounds lowers the number. A line whose round failed for a prompt too long for the model begins a new session, which the round is given again in once the work is let on.
Why: A session begun anew for its size held nothing of what the line's cell had read and done but what its loading cell read into it again: work 20's engineer, its session replaced at 808,765 tokens, went on from a session that held none of what it had read and done over its build. Compacted, the cell goes on in its own session with a summary of it and its round's whole message (decision `a-compaction-reopens-the-session-under-its-id`). Left to itself the program compacts near the model's whole window, a million tokens, so a replacement by the engine always came first; and it keeps the number for no session it resumes, so every round is given it. The number is the installation's alone: `--autocompact` in `agents.args` is refused, so that one place holds it and the engine knows the context its agents run at (principle one-thing-one-place, Article 2). What it gives up: a round before a compaction can be forked no more, the session holding only a summary of what came before (decision a-fork-begins-at-the-end-of-its-round), so the number is also how much of a long line's rounds stay open to a fork. Lowering the number to the line the history of the line's other sessions gave was turned down: judging by history (principle `judged-by-own-history`) is for watching the system's health and for a guard that stops a case its history does not explain, and a session larger than its line's earlier ones is no fault; in work 11 it replaced the session of the line `code_reviewer` at 103,764 tokens, its line being 81,768, where the installation's number was 500,000. A session that holds a prompt too long would be too long for every round after it.

### decision the-projects-files-as-the-work-has-them
A session's system prompt is made as the session begins, with the project's files (its guide, its constitution, its tree's rules) read from the work's worktree by the paths the declaration of the work's project, `flow/project.toml` on its main, names, never through a symbolic link; it is kept with the session in the data directory, and every round of the session, however it begins in it, is given the kept one. The engine does not start where main, as the host last gave it, lacks one of the files or holds one through a link; and a round whose worktree lacks one of the files fails.
Why: The owner ruled that a change of these files made in a work counts in that work: they are the owner's to change (principle protected-files-are-the-owners), and a change of them on a work's branch is meant to hold there. What keeps a work from changing the rules that govern its own agents is that the files are protected, not where the prompt is read from; read from main instead, a change the owner made on the branch would not reach the work. A link is refused because it would carry into the prompt a file the protection does not cover. The prompt is kept, since the worktree changes within a session, by the work's own commits and by main taken in, and a session keeps its system prompt as it began (principle the-system-prompt-holds-what-a-line-shares). A file missing is said rather than left out, since a session without its project's rules would look like one with them (principle nothing-passes-for-normal).

### decision the-form-is-kept-as-the-system-prompt-is
The form of return a session's program is given with every call is made as the session begins, from the flow installed then, and kept with the session beside its system prompt, by the same means; every call of the session is given the kept one, and only a new session one made anew.
Why: The form is the system prompt sent another way, and a session is given one form on every call (principle one-form-of-return-for-a-session): made anew for each round from the flow installed, it changed within a session as soon as another flow was installed. Keeping it by a means of its own beside the system prompt's was turned down, as two ways of keeping what a session began with would drift apart (principle one-thing-one-place).

### decision lines-say-where-sessions-begin
No cell begins a new session of its line at a place of its own: a cell that must not go on in another's session is on a line of its own.
Why: Lines say which cells share a session; a place within a line where its session began anew was turned down as a second way of saying the same thing, with a mark of its own on the chart.

### decision a-compaction-reopens-the-session-under-its-id
A compaction of a line's session by the agents' program during a round is recorded as the session reopened, once for each compaction, while the round is under way, and the session goes on under its id.
Why: A compacted session holds a summary where it held what the line read, so it is a session reopened (document data-and-context, 15, 26): on a line whose reading cell watches the line's `session`, that cell runs again by the rule that runs any cell, a new version of what it watches, only if the compaction is a new version of that datum; on another line, the next cell entered reads the tree first, as in any session that has not (25). The program keeps the session under its id, so a new id would have the next round begin a session that exists (decision a-lost-session-keeps-its-id).

### decision a-fork-keeps-the-sessions-tools
A fork of a round's session is given the command line of the session's last round whole, its tools among it, and a hook refuses each use of a tool but the program's own for the return; the tool list is never taken away.
Why: The tools stand at the front of every request, before the system prompt and the messages, as the form does (principle one-form-of-return-for-a-session), and the cache is read only as far as a request begins as the session's did; a fork given no tools reads none of its session from the cache and pays for all of it again. A hook that refused the return's tool too would leave the fork no way to answer in the return's form.

### decision a-fork-runs-under-its-sessions-account
A fork runs under the account its session's last try ran under, given its token; where that account was removed, under the account in use, and the fork says which was removed.
Why: A cache is one account's, and a fork is worth its cost only where it reads its session from the cache (decision a-fork-keeps-the-sessions-tools); a removed account's token is let go of, so the fork can go on only under the one in use, missing the cache, which it says so that its cost is not read as a fault.

### decision a-fork-begins-at-the-end-of-its-round
Where the session went on past the round asked of, the fork begins at that round's last message, as the stream of the round's latest try that names one names it, and holds nothing that came after; it is refused where the session was compacted or begun again under its id after that round. The cache it reads is counted from the first call after the round, a ping or the next round's start, where that came within the cache's life, else from the round's end; its spend is the program's total less all the session cost before, as the program counts a session it goes on with from all of it, whatever message it begins at.
Why: A fork asked of a round must hold only what that round saw: begun at the session's end, a fork of work 11's first plan held its later reviews, the owner's answers and the whole build, and could not show how that plan was written. After a compaction or a loss the program loads none of the session's earlier messages, and refuses to begin at one of them. The first call after the round read what the round left from the cache and wrote the cache on past it, so no later call read that again (a judgment, not measured). Taking from the total only what the session cost up to the round was turned down: the program's total counts the whole session, and a fork of that plan was told as $12.28 where it cost about $0.65.

### decision a-fork-of-an-ended-work-stands-in-for-its-worktree
Where a work has ended and its worktree is gone, a checkout of its kept branch, on no branch, is made at the worktree's place for a fork of one of its sessions, and taken away after, also when the fork is given up before its end, the work held until it is; a checkout on no branch found there is taken away after the fork as well, while the work's own worktree, on its branch, is left to the work's end; a work that has not ended and has no worktree is refused.
Why: The program finds a session by the directory it ran in, and an ended work's worktree is let go (decision letting-go-at-the-end) while its branch is kept. A checkout left behind would be taken by the next fork for the worktree and stay, and would stop a reopened work's start, which takes only a worktree on its branch; the work's own worktree is the end's to let go, and taking it away beside it would race it. A checkout, not an empty directory, so that the program sees a repository as the session's rounds did (a judgment, not measured); and only for an ended work, since a directory at the place of one that goes on would be taken for its worktree by its next round.

### decision a-forks-spend-is-kept-with-its-answer
A fork's spend is said to whoever asked it and kept with what it was told, its stream and its answer beside its round's, not put in the work's records.
Why: A fork is a look of whoever plans the work at one round's words, asked of the engine apart from the work's own way through the flow; its stream holds what it read and cost, which a later look reads there.
