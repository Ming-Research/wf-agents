Decision: The architect runs as wakes: a wake is a work of the flow's architect graph that belongs to no item, on the flow version the engine runs; one wake runs at a time, it holds no slot, and it goes from waking (A0) through the first five questions, picking and handling matters one at a time, to its wrap-up and its end (A12), instead of a round of the architect started for each event, because the architect must know how the whole stands before it takes on anything, and a wake that answers the first questions once and then handles every matter waiting does that without asking them again for each one; a wake holds no slot because the one architect over every project is not one of the works the machines' slots count.

Decision: What the architect is to handle is a matter in a queue of its own (`architect_matters`): an engineer's question at cell 15, the owner's words or answer, the owner's approval of the changes it collected, a design card to look at, a tool that failed, main's environment that changed, a side report, a place its fifth question found to change, an advance the engine asks for, a next look it set and a new policy; the engine starts a wake while none runs and a matter is due, and the sixth question picks among those waiting by the constitution and the policy, not by the order they came, instead of matters handled first come first served or a wake for each, because the architect, not the engine, judges what is most urgent, and matters that come while it is awake wait for it.

Decision: An engineer's question to the architect stops the work at cell 15, holding no slot, until the architect replies with `engine_op reply`, which takes the reply back unchanged; a reply that needs changes the owner must approve first is given once they are approved and written, instead of the owner answering in the architect's place, because the architect now runs; a question the owner was already asked in the architect's place before it ran is still answered by the owner.

Decision: A side report is queued once as a matter of the kind its own kind is closest to; an urgent one is due at once and wakes the architect, any other an hour after it came, instead of every report waking the architect, because a report that does not block its work can wait for the next wake, and an hour bounds how long.

Decision: A design card goes to the architect before the owner: it is queued as a matter, the architect rules on each decision with `engine_op rule_design_card` in the same shape as the owner's ruling, a decision it disagrees with reaches the work's engineer as an interjection as the owner's does, and the merge waits until the architect has ruled and the owner's ruling confirms every decision, the owner's word going where the two differ, instead of the owner's confirmation alone, because the architect sees whether a design fits the whole, and both must have gone through the same card before it merges.

Decision: The owner's cells of the architect's graph (reading a study, approving a policy, and answering what the architect asks) are asks of the owner, which the wake waits for holding no slot; an ask from the eighth question holds only its matter: the wake does not wait, the architect goes on to pick another, and the owner's answer comes back to the queue as a matter of its own, as the answers to the choices the fourth question leaves open do, instead of every ask stopping the wake, because the owner may take hours to answer and the other matters need not wait for one.

Decision: The architect changes the database only with the owner's approval: the changes it decides are collected in its return, put to the owner in one ask at the wrap-up (approved all, some or none, saying why), and written by the architect with `db_write`, which writes them only when they are word for word those approved; what changes nothing in the database (pausing and resuming a work, stopping and releasing new works, replying, interjecting, ruling on a design card) it does at once with `engine_op`, instead of the architect writing as it decides, or the engine checking its changes, because the owner wants every change to the items and the plan put together before it is made, and the engine has no judgment to check them with.

Decision: The architect's session goes on from one wake to the next, and the engine counts what it holds from the rounds of every wake that used it; the first wake, and the first after a new policy took effect, start a new session, and the new architect that reads a policy is on no line, a session of its own for every round, instead of a new session each wake, because the architect's understanding of the whole is what lets it judge, and a reader of the policy that knew anything but the text would not test the text.

Decision: New works stop starting while the architect holds them (`engine_op hold_dispatch`), while it sets the whole straight after its fifth question, and while it sets a new policy, each a row of `dispatch_holds`, and works already running go on, instead of a single switch, because each hold ends on its own: the architect releases its own, setting the whole straight ends when its matters are handled, and a new policy ends when it takes effect or the owner says it need not change.

Decision: A next look the architect sets at its wrap-up is a time or an item's work ending, which the engine queues as a matter due then, instead of a time or a condition written as text, because the engine must tell mechanically when it is due.

Decision: The engine asks the architect to advance when fewer items are ready to start than there are slots, at most once an hour while such a matter waits or was handled; provisional: the line is the slots' number until how long slots stand empty has been measured, and the hour keeps an empty queue from waking the architect again and again.

Rejected:
- The owner answering for the architect: rejected now that the architect's flow runs; it stands only for questions asked before.
- A wake per event: rejected because each would answer the first questions again for one matter.
