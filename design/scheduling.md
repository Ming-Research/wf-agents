# Scheduling

How works share the slots, and the owner's pause of everything.

### decision slots-bound-agents-rounds
A slot is taken by a round of an agent while it is under way, and by nothing else: a work at rest, waiting for a person
or a tool, holds none, and the engine's own acts take none.
Why: A slot stands for an agent's program running and spending, which is what there is too little of; a work that held
its slot while it waited for the owner kept another from going on, and an act that took one would hold an agent back
while it waited for CI on the host, where the repository's lock already sets the acts one after another.

### decision slots-in-the-scheduler
The engine gives a round of an agent only when a slot is free, and the walk of the work says that it waits for one;
what carries the rounds out bounds nothing of its own.
Why: A round given and then held back by its carrier until a slot came free was recorded as under way while it had not
begun, the page showed it running, and which work went first was the carrier's order and not the engine's.

### decision begun-works-first
A slot that comes free goes to a work that has begun a round before one that has not, each in the order of its number;
and a work whose round ends keeps its slot for the round it begins next, if it begins one at once, before any work that
waits.
Why: What was begun is carried on before something new begins, so that fewer works stand half done and meet each
other's changes late; and a work that goes straight on has its session's cache warm, which a work waiting its turn after
it would let go cold (principle keep-what-was-paid-for). Handing the slots round in turn, round by round, was turned
down for both.

### decision pause-of-everything-is-the-installations
The owner's pause of everything is kept as one record of the installation, beside the works and not in them, and it
holds back exactly what a work's own pause holds back: a run that answers a note still has its rounds. Lifting it lets
no work on that its own pause holds.
Why: Written into each work as a pause of its own, it would be the same fact in many places, a stop halfway through
writing it would leave some works paused and others not, and lifting it would have to tell its pauses from the owner's.
A work the owner paused alone usually has a problem and is being held, which lifting everything is not meant to undo.

### decision pause-is-no-zero-slots
Stopping every work is a pause of its own, and the slots are never set to none.
Why: With no slot, a work could not answer the owner's note to one of its cells, and works waiting for a slot could not
go on once something else freed one; a count of slots is the installation's setting, and a pause is an act of the
owner's that is set and lifted.

### decision no-exception-under-the-pause
A work's own letting on does not take it through the pause of everything: it goes on only once the pause is lifted.
Why: A work's records and the installation's have no order between them, so a letting on could not be told to come
after the pause rather than before it; ordering them by the clock would rest a decision on the time two acts were taken,
which a clock set back can reverse.
