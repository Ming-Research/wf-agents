Decision: Each project has an architect of its own, which judges only that project: a wake is a work of the flow's architect graph for one project (`works.project`), on no item, holding no slot, and each project has its own queue, holds on new starts, session line, state of things, notes, log, policy and observations; one wake of a project runs at a time, and wakes of several projects may run together; architects message one another as matters of kind `other_architect` in the receiver's queue, instead of one architect over every project, because each project's intent is its own constitution and policy, and an architect that holds one project in its context judges it better than one that holds them all.

Decision: A wake goes from `W0` (what woke it) to `W1`, which sends a session that has not understood the project since it started or was last compacted through `U1` (what the project is now), `P1` (does the policy in force still hold) and `N1` (what to do next under the policy), and one that has straight to the queue at `Q0`, instead of answering the first questions at every wake, because the understanding a session holds is what lets it judge, and asking for it again in a session that holds it costs the loop's context and adds nothing.

Decision: The three questions a wake starts with are kept apart, each a stage of its own: `U1` (understand) writes facts only, each line of work with where it stands, where it is stuck, what came of it lately and how it knows, what changed since the last time, and what it could not settle, which the owner is also told as a message; `P1` (policy, which it opens) judges only whether the policy in force still holds and its direction is right, against the constitution article by article and the signals the engine measures, holding or setting it again; `N1` (next), after the policy, thinks out from the policy's direction and principles, by first principles, what the direction needs that nothing does yet (each queued as a matter that makes it an item or records it as an observation), what matters most and why (which the ranking follows), and where what runs or waits does not fit, with its verdict (fits, some to change, set straight, keeping the holds those set); instead of an understanding that also wrote where the project heads, which earlier judgments no longer match and what each line serves, because facts mixed with judgments of the direction and of the arrangement could not be told apart, and the thinking about what to do next is the architect's, never written into the policy.

Decision: `N1` hands in one row for each thing missing and each place to set straight, with every step it means to take there, steps that depend on each other in one row, and the engine queues each row as one matter, whose path (`H1`) takes all its steps in one round, a change naming an item an earlier change of the round creates by its key, instead of a row for each step, because in a trial one place (two items for a stage of the policy, ranked before another item, with a dependency) became four matters, each costing a pick and a round of its own, and the later ones had to refer to items the earlier ones made.

Decision: When the architect's line would start a new session in the middle of a wake (the owner asked for one, the last was past the limit, the model changed, or a new policy took effect and ended the session) at a cell past the understanding, the wake goes back to `W1`, which sends it to `U1`, and a matter picked but not handled goes back to the queue, by a row every such cell of the line has (`session_anew`, which the line's `anew` gives them and the engine takes in place of the cell's round, recording why the session would start afresh), instead of the new session taking that cell, because a new session holds no state of things and no notes: one started at a matter's path moved an item against the owner's earlier request, which only the architect's notes held.

Decision: What the architect handles is a matter in its project's queue (`architect_matters`), of one of eight kinds, each with its path: `set_straight` (`H1`), `engineer_question` (`H2` then `H3`), `report` (`H4` then `H5`), `design_card` (`H6`), `failure` (`H7`), `owner_words` (`H8`), `other_architect` (`H9`) and `slots_free` (`H10`), with a `detail` that says more (an engineer's question's own kind, a side report's kind, the owner's words' kind); `Q0` takes the lone matter at once, or `Q1` picks one among several by the constitution and the policy, and an engine router sends it down its path, instead of one cell for every matter, because each kind needs other material and other judgment, and a path cell given only its matter's material keeps the loop's context small.

Decision: The queue and the batch are standard-context inputs, so that a session is given each once and after that only what changed in it, each time with the numbers of the matters that can be picked and of those the batch handled, so that it loses track of none; a path cell reads its matter's material, the items it concerns and nothing broad; the broad inputs (the board, the numbers, the overview of observations, the policy, the constitution) go to `U1`, `P1` and `N1`, the numbers measured up to the wake's start so that they hold through it; and a question's second cell (`H3`, `H5`) refers to its first cell's answer, as the cells that judge and correct a policy being set (`P7`, `P8`, `P10`) refer to the return that wrote it, or to the text the engine put together from a correction once they were given it, instead of being given it again, because the owner requires the doing loop's context to stay small.

Decision: A correction of the policy being set (`P8`) hands in only what it changes, each passage as the text has it with what it becomes, and a principle or signal added or removed, and of the probes it names those it keeps unchanged and writes out whole only those it adds or changes, a probe neither kept nor written out being removed; the engine puts the text and the probes together (`P5`), refusing a correction one of whose passages is not in the text exactly once, or that keeps a probe the version does not have, naming it, before applying any; a new architect's reading (`P6`) restates the direction and each principle in a sentence and puts its effort into the probes, instead of the whole text handed in again at each correction and every point restated at length, and instead of every probe written out again at each correction, because the reading test cost 81% of setting a policy in a trial at Sonnet ($2.66 of $3.29), and its cost was the output: 10,000 to 12,000 tokens a correction, 6,000 to 12,000 a reading; in a later trial retyping all nine probes was most of each correction's 9,000 to 16,000 tokens of output, and the main cause of its failed runs.

Decision: A reading of the policy being set passes when it answers every probe rightly, for the right reason, and fails only when it answers one wrongly or could not answer one; its restatement and its questions fail it not, and the judgment of it (`P7`), given every reading of this version with its own judgment of each, counts a question as a gap of the text when two or more readers of the version asked it or when it led a reader to a wrong answer, and writes such gaps down for the next correction, which fixes them; a reading whose answers are right while some wording should change that the reader read rightly anyway, such as a bare number that does not say whether it is a principle's or an article's, hands in the edits as a minor fix (`minor_fix`), which the engine puts into the text (`P5`, its row `fixed`) without a new reading, the reading counting as passed and the version staying the one the passes are counted for; instead of a reading failed by its questions and judged alone, and of each such fix costing a correction, two new readings and two judgments, because in a trial every reader first asked the same question, which each judgment, seeing one reading, called a judgment call, the same text unchanged was judged once passed and once failed, and the loop never ended.

Decision: The text of a policy being set is corrected at most three times since it was written, the flow's own limit, which no project's configuration sets: a reading that leaves gaps after the third correction (the engine's value `corrections`) goes on with them (`P7`'s row `gaps_left`) to the question whether the draft would need setting again at once (`P10`) and to the owner (`P11`), who is given the gaps still open beside the draft and the readings, and approves this version, sends the text back for one more correction (`fix_text`, to `P8`, given the owner's words), or has the direction studied again (`not_good_enough`, to `P2`); instead of a loop that ends only when its judgment finds no gap, and of a fault of the text alone sent back as a new study, because in a trial the loop ran six corrections without ending, the counter of corrections being only counted, and a text that needs one more fix costs one correction, while a study costs the whole loop again.

Decision: The study of a new policy (`P2`) is given the project's design goal, where the owner's own words on where the project goes are, beside the constitution, and the writing of its text (`P4`) the constitution it rests on, instead of either inferring them from what the session happens to hold, because in a trial the first study said the owner had given no words on the direction, which were in the design goal all along, and the writing had to rest the text on a constitution it was not given.

Decision: An engineer's question stops the work at cell `A2`, holding no slot, on a blocker the architect holds (design/flow/blockers.md), until the architect's reply, which the engine takes back unchanged, or until the architect hands it on with the reply's `hand_over`, to the owner or to a related project's architect, instead of asking the owner itself and waiting; a reply that waits for item changes of its round is held until the review of their batch lets them be made, and then goes with the numbers of the items made, instead of the owner answering in the architect's place, because the architect runs, and an engineer must not go on from an item that is not there yet.

Decision: A path cell hands in, with its answer, effects of one shape: item changes, which are checked as they come (`arch_changes::validate`, a failure going back to the cell) and collected in the wake's batch, and which may name an item an earlier change of the batch creates by its key, the engine giving it its number as it makes the batch in order; observation changes, actions (pause or resume a work, hold or release new starts, interject), messages to other architects and notes, which apply at once and are logged; after each matter `B1` goes back to the queue until no matter can be taken or twenty were handled, and the batch's item changes, if it collected any, go to `B2`, a review on its own line in a new session, whose findings `B3` revises, three looks at most, after which what is still wrong is dropped and put to the owner in one ask holding the matters it came from; `B4` then rethinks the whole after the batch, instead of the owner approving every change, because the owner wants the changes made by a reviewed judgment and reads them in the log, and only what the review cannot settle needs the owner.

Decision: An ask of the owner from a path cell holds only its own matter, recording the cell that asked (`resume_cell`): the path ends for now and the wake goes on with the other matters; the owner's answer puts the matter back in the queue, and when it is taken again the router sends it to the cell that asked, with the answer; no other cell of a wake asks the owner (the understanding, the pick, the revision and the rethink of a batch, the wrap-up, and the paths of an engineer's question and of a failure, which hand the work's blocker on instead), and the flow check refuses a row of the architect's graph that waits but for the owner's Start (`holds`), instead of the wake waiting for the owner, because the owner may take hours to answer, and a wake that waits stops every engineer whose blocker the architect holds.

Decision: Observations are a record beside the items: something worth doing that no item does yet, with its tags from the project's fixed set (`observation_tags` in the project's file), where it came from, how many times it was reported and what it refers to; `X2`, on its own line `observer` at the tier `[agents] observer` names (`1` by default), searches the open ones for the cell that asks with `search`, and an item that takes one in closes it once its work merges, instead of findings left in notes, because what is seen and not yet worth an item must neither be lost nor become an item before its time.

Decision: The architect's session goes on from one wake of its project to the next, and the engine counts what it holds from the rounds of every wake that used it; the first wake, one after a new policy took effect (whose setting ends the session), one past the session limit, one the owner asks for and one on another model start a new session; `B2` starts one for each batch, which its reviews after revisions continue, `X2` one at the first search of each wake, the setting of a policy one of its own (line `policy`), and the new architect that reads a policy is on no line, instead of a new session each wake, because the architect's understanding of its project is what lets it judge, and a review or a reader that knew anything but what it checks would not check it.

Decision: New starts of a project stop while its architect holds them (an action), while `N1` found what runs unfit and `H1` sets it straight, and while a new policy is set (from `P1`'s set again until the setting ends), each a row of `dispatch_holds` for the project, and works already running go on, instead of a single switch, because each hold ends on its own: the architect releases its own, setting straight ends when its matter is handled, and a new policy ends when it takes effect or the owner says it stays.

Decision: The dispatch order is the architect's: each item has a rank among its project's items, which the architect's item changes set (reorder), and the engine starts ready items by rank, taking projects in turn, instead of a plan, because the architect judges what comes first by the constitution and the policy.

Decision: While a wake's batch is open, the engine starts no work on an item a change the batch collected names (the item it changes, the item a split splits or another is moved under, the items a merge keeps and absorbs), and once the changes are made or dropped the items are as `ready_items` says, instead of starting items as they stand meanwhile, because a change such as a new dependency is made only after the review of the whole batch, and a work started before it goes against what the architect decided.

Decision: The architect does not fix what it is asked about itself: a tool that failed or main's environment that changed is fixed by an item, with the check added to the known red checks meanwhile where main is red, or by the owner for what only the owner can do, and the architect replies once it is fixed, instead of the architect fixing it in its own wake, because the architect reads and judges its project and changes its code nowhere, and a fix is an engineer's work, reviewed and tested as any other.

Decision: A design card the owner has not confirmed is a matter of the architect's, which does not rule on it: `H6` looks at whether the card is sound and its defaults reasonable in the whole, and acts when they are not, such as interjecting, and otherwise leaves the card to the owner, whose confirmation of every decision alone lets the work merge; a design card matter whose card was confirmed or replaced before it was picked is closed by the engine as it looks at the queue, saying why, instead of the architect ruling beside the owner, because the owner rules on design cards.

Decision: A work that delivered a report queues, as it ends, the latest what to do next it handed in, as a `report` matter with the report's paths and the commit merged; a side report is a `report` matter of its own, one marked urgent also reaching a running wake at the top of its next round, instead of reports collected for later, because waking the architect when it is needed costs no more, and an urgent report must reach a running architect at once.

Decision: The engine queues `slots_free` when fewer of the project's items are ready to start than there are free slots, as the action `queue_slots_free` of the cell items wait at to start (`S0`), which it takes for every project as it looks for an item to start, since no work stands there; at most once in the wait the configuration sets (`agents.slots_free_wait`, an hour unless set) while such a matter waits or was handled; provisional: the wait keeps an empty queue from waking the architect again and again until how long slots stand empty has been measured.

Decision: Why the architect pauses a work or holds new starts is written in the owner's language, kept with the pause or the hold, and shown on the page; a work it paused is let go on by its resume or by the owner's Start of that work, which is told to the architect as the owner's words, instead of a third kind of pause, because what matters to the owner is who stopped the work and that it stays stopped.

Decision: The architect answers each of the owner's words in the owner's messages (`tell_owner`), saying what it made of them and what it did: at `H8` for the owner's words that come as a matter, and in the round that took them for words the owner said to a wake while a round ran, instead of the owner reading what it made of them only in its log, because the owner talks with the architect in a window of its own on the page and the conversation goes on only when every word is answered.

Decision: Everything said in a work's group reaches the project's architect, a member of the group with the owner and the engineer: what is said to it comes to its queue as the owner's words or the engineer's question; the rest (the owner's words to the engineer, what the engineer tells the owner, and the owner's answers, to the engineer's asks and to a question the architect held) it is given as information at its next pick (`Q1`) and at its wrap-up (`E1`), whichever comes first since it last was (the engine input `group_talk`), which wakes it for nothing and needs no answer, instead of a matter for each, which would wake it and spend a round for what it only needs to know, or nothing, which would leave the architect not knowing what the owner told the engineers it holds questions of; a wake whose one matter `Q0` takes has no pick, and every wake ends at its wrap-up, so the wrap-up takes what no pick took.

Decision: `E1` wraps a wake up, writing what it learned into the state of things, and `E2` ends it, the session staying for the next wake, instead of a wake that ends when its last matter is handled, because what a wake learned is what the next wake's understanding builds on.

Decision: An engineer's answer to what the architect said to its work reaches the architect's queue as a matter of the kind `report` with the detail `answer`, which `H4` and `H5` take in as they take a report, instead of a notice to the owner only, because the flow sends each answer back to its speaker, and the architect is a speaker.

Decision: Flow feedback reaches the project's architect as a matter of the kind `report` with the detail `flow_feedback`, one that holds the feedback of every round by graph and cell until a wake picks it, feedback after that starting another; it wakes no architect, and a wake picks it only when it came before the wake started, so that feedback that comes during a wake waits for the next; `H4` judges what of it holds, and `H5` takes in what belongs to the project, and what belongs to wf-agents itself (the flow's cells, inputs, fields and texts) too when the project is wf-agents, the one whose repository wf-agents is built from, forwarding it otherwise in messages to that project's architect, which reach its queue as matters of the kind `other_architect` with the detail `flow_feedback` and, like a project's own, wake no architect and wait for a wake that started after they came, instead of a matter for each round or a wake of its own, because nearly every round hands in feedback, which the architect judges best a cell at a time and at a wake something else needed, and feedback forwarded is no more urgent than feedback handed in.

Decision: A fault of wf-agents that the owner forwards to a project's architect, by hand or by the setting that forwards each new one as it comes, reaches its queue as a matter of the kind `report` with the detail `fault` that names the engine's notice of it (`architect_matters.notice`), once for each architect; unlike flow feedback it wakes the architect as any matter in its queue does, without breaking into what it is doing, and `H4` and `H5` take it in as a report. Its material is gathered when it is handled: the notice's text and kind, the engine's evidence (for a cache miss the `cache_misses` rows behind the notice, to which a further miss with the same cause adds while the matter waits; for a flow anomaly the ask, with the owner's answer once given) and who forwarded it, the owner or the owner's setting. Instead of a copy of the notice taken when it was forwarded, because the misses that come after the forward are the same fault, and the architect judges a fault best whole.

Rejected:
- One architect over every project: rejected because each project has its own intent and the context of all of them is too large to judge one.
- The owner approving each batch of item changes: rejected; a review in a new session checks them, and the owner reads them in the log.
- The architect ruling on design cards beside the owner: rejected because the owner rules on design cards; the architect only looks at whether a card and its defaults hold.
- A wake per event: rejected because each would understand the project again for one matter.

Decision: The policy is written in the owner's language (the setting `owner.language`) in four parts: its direction (where to take the project in the near-to-middle term, and how to tell it got there), why (with the articles of the constitution it rests on), the principles for judging what matters and what comes first, from which `N1` thinks out what to do next, and the signals that the direction may have to change; it names no item, no order of work, no hold or release and no number the owner did not give, and the engine refuses one that names an item; `P2` studies the direction the owner wants and offers directions to choose from, never orders of work; the probes give a situation and ask which way the policy leans and why; instead of a policy of a goal, numbered points, stages and its own scope, written in English, because a policy that says to do items 1, 2 and 3 next is a plan already thought out, which leaves the architect nothing to think, while a direction and its principles, such as making the system keep itself up by its own flow, leave it to find from them what is missing and what matters most; and the owner reads and approves it in the owner's own language.

Decision: What a policy governs (which way the project's own work goes in the near-to-middle term, and the principles for judging what matters and what comes first) and what it never governs (the constitution, the owner's rulings on design cards, the decisions in the design tree, the flow definition and the rules, which only the owner changes, and any other project) is one rule of the flow, `policy_scope`, given to every cell given a policy in force or being set as an input, which the compiler checks (a rule's `givenWith` names the inputs it goes with, and a cell given one of them without the rule is refused); `P7` and `P10` find a draft that oversteps it a gap, and `P1` a policy in force that does a reason to set it again; instead of a scope written in the policy, because a policy that says what it governs governs itself, and deleting that part would void it, and every cell that uses a policy must know where it ends, not only the cell that writes it.

Decision: The setting of a new policy is a task of its own, a work of the graph `policy` for the project on its line `policy`, which `P1`'s `set_again` starts (one at a time for a project) while the wake goes on with its queue under the policy in force, new starts held: it studies the direction (`P2`, given the set-again reasons and the state of things from the database, its session holding neither), stops for the owner to read the study (`P3`) and to approve the text (`P11`) on a blocker the owner holds, and ends (`E1`) when the policy takes effect (`P12`) or the owner says the policy in force stays, putting a matter of the kind `policy` in the architect's queue; that matter's path is what to do next (`N1`), and a new policy that took effect ends the session the wakes go on in, so that the next round understands the project afresh, judges the new policy and thinks what to do next under it, the look at the queue closing the matter once `N1` answered since it came, instead of the wake setting the policy itself and stopping for the owner, because a wake stopped for the owner stops every engineer whose blocker the architect holds, while a policy can be set beside the work that goes on.
