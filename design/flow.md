Decision: `flow/flow.json` is the only definition of the flow, written in English, and it says nothing about any project: every project-specific value is a slot the project's configuration fills, because the same flow must run any project, wf-agents included.

Decision: The flow is compiled by the Rust crate `flow`, which the engine and the command line both use: it expands the shared rows, the gates and the asking block, builds each cell's return schema for each line, and runs every check on the flow (formats and names, every state the engine can be in is reached, each possible return takes exactly one row, every recorded value is read, every loop has a counter, every input has a source, every rule is used, the samples match); the expanded flow and its hash are stored as a flow version, because a flow that fails a check must never reach a running work.

Decision: A field of a return counts as read when a cell's input, a row, a check, an action or the cell's handoff reads it, when the cell marks it as shown on the item, which the owner's page then shows, or when the flow says which role an agent may ask reads it outside the flow, through the database, as the architect reads side reports; a field read in none of these ways is taken out of the return; what a cell does every time it returns is an action, never a database view the field is written into, because the owner reads the item and the architect the side reports, while a view named only as a note let a field nothing reads pass the check that every field is read.

Decision: The language each field of an agent's return is written in is said in the return's schema, and in no rule: every field is in English, as is everything that goes into the repository (commit messages, the PR's title and description); a field shown on the item, the design card with what changed from the card it replaces, and what an ask of the owner says (each point's what and what was tried, and the choice) are in the owner's language, `owner.language`, with technical terms in their English original, because the agent writes a field by its description, and what is read in the repository and by agents is English while what the owner reads is in the owner's language.

Decision: A design card that replaces an earlier one of its work says, in a sentence or two the round that hands it in writes beside it, what changed from the card it replaces and why, and the engine keeps it with the new card, instead of the owner comparing the two cards, because cards that replace one another look alike and the owner reads a replaced card only as what replaced it.

Decision: The owner rules on each decision of a design card, confirming it, disagreeing with it saying why, or choosing another of its approaches, and sends the verdicts on all of them in one ruling; the card is confirmed only when every decision is, and the decisions disagreed with reach the work each with the approach chosen and why, instead of one verdict on the whole card, because a card holds several decisions and the owner may disagree with one of them only.

Decision: A work is pinned to the flow version it started with, and is never moved onto another; when the flow moves to a new version, a work on an older one stops when it next goes on and its item starts again on the new one (design/engine/half-done-work.md), because changing the flow under a running work could leave it in a cell or a state the new flow no longer has, and a work that ran on to its end would follow a flow that is no longer the flow.

Decision: The flow has two graphs, the engineer's, which a work on an item runs, and the architect's, which each wake of a project's architect runs, and they meet only through the database: a question to the architect, a side report and a design card become matters in the queue of the item's project's architect, and the architect acts on the works with the effects of its returns, instead of one graph or a row from one graph into the other, because an architect runs for a long time over every work of its project at once, while each work runs on its own, and the compiler checks each graph on its own.

Decision: The architect's graph is the port of the design the owner approved before wf-agents existed, cell for cell and row for row (twenty-six cells, eighty-two rows, as many states as that design had), written in the flow's present form; where wf-agents decided otherwise (items own no item, the dispatch queue, design cards ruled per decision, the owner's two names for what the owner is put before), the port follows wf-agents, and every such choice was put to the owner with the page, instead of a new design, because the owner approved that design and a port that changes what a cell decides would be a different flow.

Decision: Rules every graph shares are written once, at the top of the flow, each naming the lines of any graph it is given to, and a graph's own rules after them, instead of each graph repeating a rule, because the general rules hold for the architect as for the engineers, and a rule written twice drifts.

Decision: A gate answers with one of two named outcomes, the one that goes on, which it hands in as its answer with why and whose row is named after it, and the problem, which it asks about with the gate's kind; its title names the topic, such as `R2` "Whether it stands" with `stands` and `does_not_stand`, instead of yes or no to a question, because a gate asked as the problem made "no" the way on, which read wrong on the chart, and a question asked the other way round leaves which answer goes on as unclear.

Decision: Every agent's cell has three kinds of row: its own answers; the moves its agent chooses that every agent's cell shares (tools, search, the asks, a redirect to `A4`, a pause and answers to an interjection), each a row to a cell of its own that comes back (`X1`, `X2`, `A1` to `A3`, `X3` waiting for the owner's Start, `X4` handing the answers on); and the engine's fallback, rows the compiler gives every agent's cell from the rounds section and marks as such (`fallback`), for a return sent back, a failed run and the engine's own cases (a round cut off, a step it cannot take, a message it does not send, a work that ended meanwhile), which no return takes and the engine carries out itself; instead of a pause and answers going back to the cell itself and the fallback mixed with the shared rows, because a pause waits for the owner and answers reach their speakers, which the flow then shows where they happen, and the fallback is the same for every cell, so the chart leaves it out and the cell's table lists it apart.

Decision: The flow's engine section names the places the engine keeps what it writes (`stores`), each in its layer, a project, an item, or a work and its rounds, with the tables and views that hold it and whether it is computed from the rounds; what each action and each step that writes one itself writes (`writes`); and what each of the engine's values reads, with a few words the chart says under its cell (`reads`); the compiler refuses an action that names no stores, a value that does not say what it reads, and a store, table or view that is not there, instead of these being found only in the engine's code, because the chart draws who writes and reads each store from them (Article 7), and a table the check keeps whole cannot drift from the actions.

Decision: An item waits to start at cell `U0`, the first of the engineer's graph, at the stage `unready`, whose rows say what holds it back (held, blocked by an item not merged, its project's new starts held, named by the architect's open batch, no slot) and lead on to `S0` when nothing does; no work stands there, since no work exists while an item waits, so the engine takes its rows for every item that waits and makes a work, at the cell the row leads to, for the one it starts; the choice among items ready stays the engine's, as the cell's step says. A work cancelled or taken over ends at cell `C1` from any cell (`cancel`), keeping the cell it stopped at, and what the engine does at an end, or at a cell before its step, is the cell's own actions (`do`), instead of the conditions of a start and the shared ending written only in the engine, because how a work starts and ends is behaviour (Article 7).

Decision: A jump the engine made out of the flow's sight is a row: the architect's line going back to `W1` when a new session would start in the middle of a wake (`session_anew`); a work behind another in its project's merge queue waiting its turn at `M2`, holding no slot (`behind`); and an item a work waited for cancelled, at `A2`, `A6` and `M1`, taken to choose the way on (`cancelled`, by the notice the cancel leaves the work), instead of the engine moving or holding the work itself, because a work that went wrong is traced by its rows.

Decision: The total time is measured, not counted: the time of a work's agents' rounds and of the tools they had the engine run, in minutes (the view `work_counters`), and its limit in the project's configuration is the budget of one stretch, the time since its agents last handed in pause; once a stretch's budget is used up, the engine asks the agent to pause (after its next tool use, or at the top of its next round), and the pause waits at `X3` for the owner's Start, which begins the next stretch; every other counter past its limit is told to the owner once, as it passes, and the work goes on; instead of the rows that pause counting the total time, because a count of pauses is no time, and a limit no one is told of limits nothing.

Decision: When the owner's ruling disagrees with a decision of the design card and the owner's approach changes the acceptance, the design that follows the ruling (`D2`) writes the new acceptance without asking the advisor, and the engine locks it after design as any design's, instead of the ruling stating the acceptance, because acceptance conditions are written in the engineer's form (numbers, tests or measurements, criteria), which the owner's ruling on approaches does not and should not ask of the owner.

Decision: A row of an engine step that takes an interjection, as `T7`'s, sets aside what the step handed in (the checks it chose), which the step works out again if the way on comes back to it, and the compiler counts it as dropping nothing, instead of an action on the row that does nothing but read what is set aside (`set_aside_checks`), because an action that does nothing is behaviour the flow names and the engine does not have.

Rejected:
- Project names, branches or rules written into the flow: rejected because the flow would then work for one project only.
