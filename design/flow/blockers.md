Decision: Only a task stops, and only at the cell where its problem arose, on a blocker raised there: a task is an engineer's work or the setting of a policy, never a wake of the architect; what agents and the owner say to each other is asynchronous and stops no one, instead of a question stopping the side that asks until the side asked replies, because when questions stop their askers, a question passed on (an engineer asks the architect, which asks the owner) becomes a chain of sides waiting on each other, some hops stopping and some not, and nothing makes sure the chain closes; with one blocker per stopped task there is no chain, only who holds the blocker now.

Decision: A blocker belongs to the task and the cell that raised it and is held by exactly one holder at a time: the work's advisor, a project's architect, the owner, or items, whose merge resolves it; it records its kind, what the problem is, every hand-over with why and what the holder found, what is said about it, and how it ended: resolved, with what was decided and done, or withdrawn, with why. An engineer's question, an engine tool's failure, an ask of the owner, a wait for items to deliver and a wait for the owner's ruling on a design card are each a blocker, instead of each being a record of its own kind (a matter's reply, an ask, the wait at `A6`, the merge gate's wait), because one record with one holder answers, for any stopped task, what it waits for and who must act, and a holder that no longer exists is found in one place. Implemented: the table `blockers` (`db/schema.sql`; migration 36 made a blocker of every ask of an engineer's work of the owner, engineer's question and failure with the architect's reply, wait at `A6` and merge gate's wait), which the rows' actions `raise_blocker`, `resolve_blocker` and `withdraw_blocker` move (`crates/engine/src/blockers.rs`).

Decision: A holder never stops while it holds a blocker: it resolves it, or hands it to another holder, saying why and what it found. The architect that needs the owner's decision hands the blocker to the owner instead of asking and waiting, and the owner resolves it or hands it back with a decision for the architect to carry out, instead of a holder that asks another and waits for the answer, because a holder that waits stops every blocker it holds, as a wake of the architect waiting for the owner stops every engineer waiting for the architect.

Decision: Every move of a blocker is a row of a graph (Article 7): the stopped work stands at the cell of its blocker's holder (`A1` the advisor, `A2` the architect, `A3` the owner, `A6` items, `M1` items and the owner's rulings before the merge), goes from one to another by a row when the blocker is handed on (`A1` to `A2` or `A3`, `A2` to `A3`, `A3` back to `A2`), and goes to `A4`, which chooses the way on, when it is resolved; the engine moves a blocker only where a row says so, instead of the engine moving blockers between holders on its own, because the owner and the agents read the flow, and a stopped work's cell then says who must act, which is where a work that went wrong is traced from.

Decision: Taking a blocker higher is a cell of the waiting work's graph, not a timer of the engine: when a blocker at `A2` has waited past its limit, measured in the time the architect had to act on it (awake and not paused), a row takes the work to the engineer's cell "Still waiting?", which waits on (back to `A2`, the limit counted again), takes it to the owner (to `A3`, saying why) or withdraws it and goes another way (to `A4`); provisional: the limit is two hours of the architect's time until how long the architect takes has been measured. Instead of the engine handing a blocker to the owner after a time, because escalation is behaviour (Article 7): an escalation the flow draws can be read and traced, while a timer in the engine can be found only in its code.

Decision: Each cell where a work waits has a row for each way its holder can fail it and for the owner's interjection, each taking the work to `A4` with what happened (an item it waited for was cancelled; the owner said something to it), instead of a work that waits forever on a holder that is gone or that its owner cannot turn, because a cancelled item is never merged, and a work at `A2` took no interjection until its reply came.

Decision: A task's end ends its open blockers: each is withdrawn with the end's reason and its holder is told (a matter leaves the architect's queue, an ask of the owner needs no answer), and a resolution that comes for a withdrawn blocker is recorded with it and moves nothing, instead of the holder going on with a blocker no task waits on, because the architect spent a round answering the question of a work that had ended, and its reply reached no one. Implemented: `ending::end` withdraws the open blocker of every work that ends, as `E1` and `C1` name it (`withdraw_blockers`), and closes its ask or withdraws its matter; an architect's reply that comes later is said about the blocker as late.

Decision: The setting of a policy is a task of its own, a work of the graph `policy` on a line of its own, which `P1`'s verdict `set_again` starts and whose `P3` and `P11` stop only it, while the wake goes on with its queue under the policy in force; the understanding (`U1`, `P1` and `N1`) hands what is unclear to the owner as a message and records it as unclear in the state of things, instead of stopping the wake, because a wake stopped for the owner stops every engineer whose blocker the architect holds, while a policy can be set beside the work that goes on.

Decision: The flow check holds the graphs to this: no cell on a holder's path stops for anything, and every cell where a work waits on a blocker has a row for its resolution, for each way its holder can fail it and for the owner's interjection; the engine's tests run random sequences of events (asks, hand-overs, answers, interjections, cancels, ends, takeovers) and check after each that every stopped task has one open blocker raised at the cell it stands at, every open blocker has a holder that exists, no ended task has an open blocker, and the items waited for form no cycle, instead of reading each new path to judge whether it closes, because these properties are what make every chain close, and a cell or a row that breaks one is then refused before it runs. Implemented for the engine: `engine::blockers::invariants`, which the tests `every_chain_of_blockers_closes_under_random_events_*` (`crates/engine/tests/e2e.rs`) check after every event.

Decision: The owner's conversations are read from the blockers and what is said: a work's conversation holds its blockers' moves and what was said about them, and what is pinned in a conversation is the open blockers the owner holds, instead of a record of its own for the page, because the page then shows what the engine acts on.

Decision: An ask of the owner (`owner_asks`) stays the record of what is put before the owner and of the owner's answer, and a blocker the owner holds names its ask (`blockers.ask`), as one an architect holds names its matter in the architect's queue (`blockers.matter`), instead of the blocker holding the question's text and the answer itself, because the owner's page answers every ask one way, whether it holds a task or only a matter of the architect's, and the architect's queue is where an architect picks up what it holds; the blocker is the one record of who holds the stop and how it moved and ended, and the ask and the matter are what it says to its holder.

Rejected:
- A chain of requests with a graph of who waits for whom, a withdrawal passed down the chain: rejected because each hop keeps its own rule for stopping, and the chain must still be followed to find who must act; with one blocker per stopped task there is no chain to follow.
- The engine taking a blocker higher after a time, or handing it on when its holder is gone: rejected by Article 7.
- A wake of the architect stopping for the owner: rejected because it stops every work whose blocker the architect holds.
