# What is said to a work

How the owner's words reach a work's cells, and how what the cells say back is kept.

### decision the-owner-speaks-to-a-line-in-one-act
The owner speaks to a line of the work's flow, in one act of the engine's: the words are the owner's entry of each
datum the cell's trigger of the owner's words has its agent read, `discussion` in the engineer's flow, and a new
version of that trigger's block, which the engine's source `words` writes, of the cell of the line the work has
reached: of the line's cells the owner's words run that apply now, the last of which a run started, else the first.
Words to a line with no such cell are refused, and that block is written by no one outside the act.
Why: Written by the page into a block of each cell's own, the record and what runs the cell were two writes the page
had to keep together, and the page chose the cell; the engine walks the work and knows which cell of the line it has
reached (design document `data-and-context`, 39). As the engine's act, the words stop a round under way of a later cell
and enter a cell that waits, as an answer does, which no write of the owner's could.

### decision words-to-a-round-under-way-go-in-through-its-hook
Where a round of the cell spoken to is under way, the version of the trigger is put into it, and its carrier puts the
words, as the message's file says them, in a file the hook the agent's program runs after each use of one of its own
tools gives the session; what the words are written into is not put in. Words that reach no round before it ends stay
new to the cell, which runs again for them.
Why: The words should not wait for the round's end (design document `data-and-context`, 41). The old engine's hook read
the store itself; this one needs only the shell and files beside the round, so that what runs the agents may be apart
from the engine. The record is left to the session's next filling, since the round may end before its hook gives the
words, and a datum put in would be counted as held.

### decision what-a-return-says-in-a-field-is-a-version-of-its-datum
What a return gives in a field of the engine's that a datum is written through is its run's version of that datum as
the run finishes: its answer, `reply`, its entry of the record of what is said; its questions, a version of `questions`
that says every question of the work that stands, in the words of that datum's file. A return that says it dealt with
the owner's words and gives no answer is sent back.
Why: A session reopened is then filled with them as with any datum, and a cell given `questions` is given the questions
other cells asked, which a field read only from the return it came in would not give (design document
`data-and-context`, 38, 44). A second way of giving them was turned down.
