### decision the-planner-uses-the-commands
Whoever plans the work reads and changes the plan with the program's commands, each a request to a route of the owner's interface whose answer the command says in bounded words; no server of tools of its own stands beside them.
Why: A server of tools for the planner, each answering one question in bounded words, was turned down: the commands do that already, over the routes the page uses, so it would be a second way to do one thing (principle one-thing-one-place), and one more that every planning session must be given. This rests on judgment.

### decision the-plans-mark-counts-what-was-read
The mark `wf-agents changes` gives is the highest item read and, for each item, how many of its changes and how many of its work's cancels and reopens were read, each counted in that item's own order; not a time. An answer gives what follows those counts, oldest first, an item's changes only once the item is read; times only order it.
Why: As with the mark of `wait` (decision the-mark-is-a-place-in-the-records), a time would give a change twice or not at all when two come in one millisecond or the clock goes back; an item's changes and its work's cancels and reopens are only added to, so a count of each is a place, and as an answer gives of each what follows its count in its own order, one cut at its bound leaves out only what the next mark gives, whatever the times say.

### decision candidates-by-what-waits-for-them
The candidates are listed with those that more open items wait for, by way of any items, first, then by number; which to let on is the planner's to choose.
Why: Counting only the open items that wait on each directly was turned down: it puts an item in the middle of a chain above the one at its bottom, which every item of the chain waits for. Choosing for the planner was turned down: works are let on by hand, so that whoever plans can put something first. This rests on judgment.
