# The console

The items as a tree in columns.

### decision the-tree-is-drawn-on-a-canvas
The console's tree is drawn in WebGL on a canvas, only what is on the screen and the titles from one shared set of
glyphs; what an item opens is HTML.
Why: So that hundreds of items move smoothly as the columns glide, which rests on judgment; measured was that a
texture for each title stalled a frame for up to 150 ms as items came into view, where one shared set of glyphs did
not. What an item opens holds words to select and copy, markdown and answers to type, which only HTML does well.

### decision the-tree-follows-what-depends-on-what
The items stand as one tree along what depends on what: where one item depends on another, the other stands under it,
directly or under an item under it. No second tree is drawn by anything else.
Why: Reading down from an item shows everything it needs, and how far it has got is how far what stands under it has
got. A tree drawn by anything besides would be a second relation to keep in step with the dependencies.

### decision an-item-stands-in-one-place
An item stands whole in one place, under the first by number of the items that depend on it; under each other item
that depends on it, it is only a line that points there.
Why: Shown whole in several places, an item would be counted, read and answered more than once, and each thing has one
place (Article 8).

### decision a-card-writes-the-lines-its-work-changed-where-its-time-went
A card writes the lines its work added and removed, `+210 −40`, on its second row past the strip, where it drew the
line of where the work's time went; an ended item's card, at the right of its one row. The added are in the green and
the removed in the red a diff draws them in. The line of where a work's time went stays in the item's window only.
Why: The second row had room for the one or the other, and the owner chose the lines; the time's line is drawn once,
in the window (Article 8). A low row under the second row for the lines was turned down: the second row had room.
Green for what was added and red for what was removed is how a diff draws them everywhere on the page; the reverse, as
prices are drawn, was turned down as one meaning in two colours (Article 8).

### decision a-card-stands-still
No card is lit or moved for its work's state: one that waits for the owner has no rings round it, and a running one no
light going round its edge. What runs is shown by the pulse of its running cell's mark, and by the way from its item up
to its root, dashed in the colour of a running card's edge, its dashes moving from the root's side to the item's.
Why: Red rings round the cards that wait for the owner, and a light going round a running card's edge, were tried and
turned down as too strong; this rests on the owner's judgment of the page (principle owner-taste-judges).

### decision what-the-owner-points-at-stands-in-a-soft-light
The item selected and the item under the pointer stand in a soft light, a blurred fill behind the card, the selected
one's wider and its ring thicker; the light and the rings of each item take its own colour, as its card shows it.
Over the light lies the page's ground at every card it reaches, and at an item drawn with no box only where the light
is its own, so that it shows only round the cards.
Why: The soft light was chosen over other marks of the selection on the owner's judgment (principle
owner-taste-judges). The ground looks needless under a card drawn whole, but without it the light showed through a
card drawn faint, as a flash of colour; it is the page's colour, and under a boxless item only for its own light,
because in a card's colour, or under another's light, it drew a box where the item has none.
