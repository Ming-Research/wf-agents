Decision: The page is built with React and Vite, and the graphs it draws are laid out by elkjs, instead of mermaid, because each node is then a component that can carry overlays and badges, such as a work's current cell and its round counts, while mermaid renders a fixed picture. The items' dependency graph is drawn by React Flow; a flow's state machine is drawn as SVG by the page's own components in a pan-and-zoom view of its own, because it draws what React Flow does not (stage boxes around their cells, arrows routed at right angles with rounded bends and an arch where two cross, small circles for rows to another part of the chart, rules and the interjection beside their cells) and is moved and zoomed as the owner's approved state-machine page was, which React Flow's own handling of the wheel and the keys does not allow.

Decision: A flow's state machine fills the window under the bar, with a legend of who does each cell's work, and what is open on it (a cell, a rule, the interjection, a comparison, a round) floats over its right side in a panel that closes, because the chart is what the owner reads the flow from, and a column kept for a panel that is often empty halves it.

Decision: A work is drawn on the state machine of the flow version it is pinned to, with its rounds placed on their cells (the current cell highlighted, each visited cell with its round count, rounds sent back or failed marked, the transitions taken emphasised), so that a round leads to its cell and a cell to its rounds, instead of on the current flow, because a work runs only on its pinned version, and the current flow may no longer have its cells.

Decision: The flow page compares two flows: two stored versions, or a stored version and the flow as `flow/flow.json` is at a git ref of a project's checkout, which the interface reads with git and compiles with the flow's compiler without writing anything; the chart of the newer is drawn with the cells added and changed marked, and a panel lists what changed in each cell, because a proposed change to the flow is reviewed against the flow it changes, on the page the flow is read on.

Decision: Every text of the page is in English, from one message catalog, because the owner reads the interface in English, and one catalog keeps the names of the flow's parts the same wherever the page shows them.

Decision: The works are one view: those running or waiting at the top, in an area four rows high that scrolls and whose height can be dragged, those ended folded under them in a list that scrolls the same way, and the work picked from either shown below with its chart and rounds, the list staying; the view has no heading of its own and its tables fill the width, instead of a list that a picked work replaced, because the owner goes from work to work, and a heading repeating the tab, or a table narrower than the window, spends room the works need.

Decision: The bar holds the views and the engine's state (running, holding, or waiting for what stops every work, until when), the slots in use and the unread messages, and nothing of a view: the state opens a panel that switches between running and holding, a work's facts are its row in the list of works, and the flow page's choice of version and comparison floats over the chart's top left corner, instead of the controls and facts of the view shown in the bar, because a control pressed from a view about something else did what the owner could not see, and a work's facts in the bar repeated its row just above.

Decision: The two lists of works have the same columns at the same widths, a work's item opens from its row, and the stage and the reason a work paused or ended are written in words in its row, the state on one line (how long a running work has run, ticking; what waits for the owner as small chips; the reason cut to the line and whole on hovering), because the two lists are read as one table, a reason prefixed with the state its badge already shows says it twice, and a row that wraps pushes the fourth work out of the four rows' area.

Decision: An item shows what its works found and decided as it stands now: where it stands (the latest progress), then each thing found or decided at its newest with the round that wrote it (a long one folded until asked for) and the earlier versions folded, then every progress note newest first; dependencies are shown only when there are any, and the dependency graph on the items view only when any item depends on another, instead of every round's values in order and empty boxes, because the owner reads an item to know where it stands, and a page of every round's values, or a graph that repeats the table below it, hides that.

Decision: A round opens as what happened, in a sentence or two (who did the work: an agent, with its time, model and cost, or no agent when the engine answered the cell itself; how it ended; where the work came from and went on to), then for an agent's round its inputs (each input of its cell with the addresses it had that round, or that it had none), what it was actually given once repeats were taken out (each given part with its content, those its session already held only named), its task folded, and what it handed back; for a step that was not an agent's, what it read and what it came to, without the progress the sentence already says; the whole message as sent is folded at the end, instead of a table of the round's stored facts (session, call, usage, config commit, raw engine input), because the owner opens a round to see what an agent was asked, what it was given and what it did, and those facts answer none of it.

Decision: A work's rounds are listed with the cell and who did its work (the agent's line, or the engine, also for a gate it answered itself because the project lacks what the gate needs, or the tools), the row taken with a badge only for what is not the usual course (sent back, failed, cut off, running), what an agent's round cost and how long it took; a round left open while the engine holds and runs nothing of the work (one the engine stopped in, or one a pause left open) shows as left open and the work as ready, so that its next round, which closes it first, can be run; a round's panel says where the work came from and where it went, and names the inputs that had nothing that round on one line by short names, instead of a badge on every round, the same look for a round no agent ran, and every input's whole description, because the owner scans the rounds for what went wrong, what cost much and which rounds really ran an agent.

Decision: The owner rules on a design card, answers an ask of the owner and lets a paused work go on (beside the reason it paused, once the owner confirms the cell that runs again) on the page, where the design card is laid out as its decisions (settled, provisional or to check; the node; what changes from what to what and why; the approaches compared with their verdicts; how sure and what would overturn it) and the ask as each point it asks about (its kind in words, what it says with any code kept as code, what was tried) and its choice with the option recommended; the page writes what the command line writes and wakes the engine, instead of only the command line, because the owner works from the page, and a decision read as a tree of JSON is not read.

Decision: The messages view lists what the engine tells the owner of its own accord (a work stopped by a new flow version, a design card posted, every work halted until a limit resets) beside what agents told the owner, marked as the engine's and counted in the unread number, a notice of a new ask saying its kind and the first sentence of what it asks and linking to the ask, kept in a table of their own that agents do not read, instead of only in the notifications log, because the owner reads the page, not the log; the table is hidden from agents so that the index of tables the flow gives them, and with it the flow's digest, stays as it is.

Decision: Text the agents and the owner wrote (an item, what a round handed back) reads in the page's font, and only a message as sent, a file and what a tool printed keep a fixed width, because that text is prose, and a fixed width makes prose slower to read.

Decision: Every chart moves and zooms alike: two fingers or the wheel move it, Shift with two fingers or a pinch zooms at the pointer, dragging moves it, and Space shows the whole and goes back, because one chart that behaves differently from the others makes every gesture a guess.

Decision: The page shows the owner only what the owner uses: a block of values the engine keeps for itself, such as a work's machine, worktree, pushed head or recorded values, is not shown, because what is drawn is there for the owner to read, and what the owner cannot use crowds out what the owner needs.
