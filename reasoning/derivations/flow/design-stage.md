Decision design-from-records: The design stage derives the design from the project's records, its cells numbered in the order they run: `D1` follows the derivations that govern the problem up to the articles and derives the solution, finding that the records decide it with one workable way (`decided`), leave several workable ways open (`trade_off`), or have no workable way without revising a record (`must_change`); a trade-off or a revision has the chain it stands on challenged (`D3`) before `D4` writes the design, the revision of the records with its entry in their log, and the cards; a project that keeps no records designs from the problem itself and goes straight to `D4`; instead of comparing at least three approaches, choosing one and asking again whether there is a better design in every work, because where the records decide a question with one workable way a comparison is time spent for nothing, and where they leave it open the comparison is a trade-off the owner rules, which the records then hold for every later work.

Rests on: C:efficient, C:information, D:reasoning-rules-by-role, D:way-cells

Rejected:
- Comparing at least three approaches, choosing one and writing the design, then asking in the engineer's own session whether there is a better design, in every work: rejected because it compares where the records leave one workable way, a round spent for nothing, and decides what the records leave open without putting it to the owner.

Decision fresh-review-sessions: The challenge of the chain (`D3`) and the coherence review (`D7`) are on the review line and each starts a new session of it every time the work comes to it, by the action `fresh_session` on the row into it, instead of going on in the session of the line that made what they judge or in their own earlier one, because a judgment made in a session that holds the reasoning behind what it judges leans on that reasoning, and a session started afresh holds none of it.

Rests on: C:information, F:session-anchors, F:fresh-session-empty

Decision records-back-to-derive: A cell that finds the records must change (the design, `D4`; implementing, `I1`; the check of the code against the records, `T5`; the fix, `T6`) hands in `records_must_change` with what it found, and the work goes through `A8`, which chooses by whether the project keeps records: back to derive again (`D1`), carrying what was found, even halfway through implementation, or, in a project that keeps none, back to the cell that found it; instead of the cell revising the records itself, because a revision made outside the design stage reaches neither the records check nor the coherence review, and a design whose records changed under it is derived again from them.

Rests on: C:information, D:design-from-records, D:records-check, D:way-cells

Decision code-against-records: In a project that keeps records, the table review is followed by a round of the review line that checks the code against them (`T5`), by the work extension's section on reviewing the work, one column for each of its points (choices governed, contradiction, orphans, commitments, the chain challenged), and it hands in `records_must_change` for a choice no derivation governs, instead of a round that reviewed the change to the design tree only when the tree changed, because the code is governed by the records whether or not the work changes them, and a missing derivation is put to the owner as a revision, not fixed as a finding.

Rests on: C:information, D:reasoning-rules-by-role, D:records-back-to-derive

Rejected:
- A round of its own for the design tree after the table review, when the change touched the tree, with the four points on code against decisions answered in the table review: rejected because it reviewed the code against the records only as one column among the table review's, and a work that left the tree alone had none of it.

Decision decision-cards: Each question a revision of the records puts to the owner is a decision card, which holds what the reasoning system's card extension says a card holds (a key that stays the same for the same question, the question, at least two workable options each with what it does, its cost and its risks, those found not workable with why, the one recommended, how sure with why and what would overturn it, where it departs from an earlier ruling, and the records it revises): the design (`D4`) writes the cards with the revision, each standing in the design's text as a line `[[card:<key>]]` where the design reaches its question; a change to the records that changes no decision (wording, form, a record moved) is listed beside the cards as an edit, saying why, and needs no card, the coherence review (`D7`) checking that it changes none; the coherence review judges each card by the card extension and, given what the project's architect knows (its plan with the owner's lasting instructions, what the project is now, the board of the project's items, the decision cards of the others, the policy in force), whether it fits the plan, the owner's instructions and the other items and is the owner's to rule at all, asking the architect by the ask every cell has where a card touches another item or the plan in a way it cannot settle, the work waiting for the answer as for any ask; the engine checks that the anchors are the cards, that every card and edit names records the branch revised and every record the branch revised is on a card or an edit, that every card of the work comes back the same, changed saying what changed, or withdrawn saying why, and that a card follows the owner's ruling on its key; the cards are posted once the coherence review and the checks after design pass, each with the records it revises as the branch has them, a card handed in again the same keeping its number and its ruling; no later cell changes the records (D:records-back-to-derive); and the merge waits until the owner confirmed every current card and the records to merge are word for word what the cards put to the owner or listed as edits, instead of one decision card for the change, a section for each decision with three approaches compared, which a later round handed in again whole, because what a card holds is the reasoning system's, which keeps it with the revision it puts, a question read where the design reaches it is read with its background, a ruling that follows a key across versions asks the owner again only what changed, and one review of a card, in the work, costs a round the work runs anyway and waits in no queue, the architect's knowledge given to it as inputs.

Rejected:
- One design card for the whole change, each decision a section with at least three approaches compared: rejected because a card of several questions is ruled as one set, and three approaches made up where two are workable fill a count.
- Cards as blocks of the design's text, parsed out of it: rejected because a card's fields are then checked by code of the engine's own instead of the flow's shapes, and every reader of the design reads a card's fields mixed into it.
- A card for every record changed, a rewording too: rejected because it puts to the owner as a question what changes no decision.
- The architect looking at each card posted (`H6`), beside the owner or before the owner sees it: rejected because a second review in another line costs the architect a round for every card, and the card either waits in the architect's queue behind other matters or reaches the owner before the look, who may rule a card the architect then sends back, or looks at for nothing (F:stale-card-look); what the architect brings is what it knows, which the coherence review is given.

Decision flow-change-ruled-redrawn: In wf-agents' own repository, the one whose flow file is the flow, one decision card of a work whose branch changes the flow carries the flow drawn from the branch: when the engine posts the cards it compiles the branch's flow file and keeps the compiled flow in the database, once by its digest, with the flow version in force then and the flow file it was compiled from, on the work's first card or the one that carries it already; when the flow on the branch changed since, as in implementation, the merge (`M1`) posts a new version of that card carrying it as the branch now has it, saying the flow changed in implementation, and waits for its ruling, the new version posted without another review, since it changes the drawing alone and the flow's change in implementation went through the work's reviews, which hold it to the design and its cards; the card leads to the flow page drawn from that copy and compared with that version, instead of a link to the branch, so that what merges is the flow the owner saw redrawn, as the owner reads the flow and as a change to the flow's behaviour is approved (D:flow-change-approval), the flow holding that rule instead of the reasons a work is held, and the drawing stays after the branch is merged or gone, whatever the compiler later becomes. Another project's file at that path is that project's own, never a change to the flow, since the flow is wf-agents' own, compiled into the program, and belongs to no project.

Rejected:
- The card leading to the flow page at the branch's ref, compiled when the page is opened: rejected because the branch is gone once the work merges or ends, and a later compiler may not read an earlier flow.
- The flow file named in each project's configuration, each project running a flow of its own: rejected because the flow is wf-agents', built into the program; a flow for each project would want a switch on the page and every project changed for a change to it.
- The flow drawn once, as the design posted its cards: rejected because wf-agents' flow is mostly changed in implementation, after the cards, and what merged would not be the flow the owner saw.
