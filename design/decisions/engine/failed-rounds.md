# Failed rounds

### decision the-fields-tell-the-kind-before-the-words
The kind of a failed round, and when a usage limit resets, are told by the fields the command line names the failure in before its words: the service's typed kinds of the error, a rate limit event that refused with its reset, why the round ended, then the status the service answered with, which gives the class (a server's fault or a timeout is the service unavailable, a refused sign-in cannot run, a 429 is the usage limit only where the words say so and else the service unavailable, any other 4xx pauses its work). The words decide only what the fields leave open, as when it crashed, and a connection that failed after the round spent tokens is the round's own failure, in passing, and not the network's.
Why: The words change between versions of the command line, and a kind told by them misses each new wording: a limit in new words is taken for a failure in passing and tried again, and a reset written with its day, as "resets Fri 5pm", is read wrong by words that know only a time of day. The status alone cannot tell the account's usage limit from a throttle, both 429, nor a context too long from another refused request, both 400, so within its class the words still choose. A service that fails, is overloaded or throttles the account fails every work's rounds alike, which the command line has already tried again itself before it says so. A cut connection, or a body that ended early, says nothing of the network once the service has served the round.

### decision an-error-nothing-tells-pauses-its-work
A round whose command line printed an error that neither its fields, the status the service answered with, nor its words tell the kind of pauses its work, with what it said; a result that is no error and holds no return does too.
Why: Taken as a failure in passing, a weekly limit in words no list held failed eight rounds in a row over two hours, each meeting the limit again; what nobody has told apart may as well be a new kind of limit every round meets, and the owner is the one to look (principle nothing-passes-for-normal). A round that printed no result is another matter: it crashed or was killed, and is tried again (decision a-crash-is-tried-again).

### decision a-crash-is-tried-again
A round whose command line printed no result, having said it began or having reached the service, fails in passing, unless its words tell another kind, and is tried again within the bound on failures in passing in a row; what it spent is its calls' tokens at their model's prices, a model not known priced as the dearest. One that did neither, as a program that could not be started, is the command line itself failing to run, whatever its words, which holds every work (decision a-shared-cause-is-one-wait).
Why: A crash says nothing of its cause, and most pass; paused, a work left to run unattended would wait for the owner for what may pass by itself, a judgment no case has measured. Counted, what each try spent reaches the work's budget, and the bound keeps the tries from going on unseen. A command line that ends before it says it began fails so for every work, and tried again in each it would fail as often as the bound lets, each work then waiting for the owner; that a crash of one session's own comes after it began rests on judgment.

### decision a-try-its-engine-did-not-see-end-ends-as-its-stream-says
A round given again after the process of the engine that gave it ended while a try of it ran goes on from that try where its program said it began, reached the service or printed its result: what the try kept of its stream is read as the end of a try that ended by itself, a return there taken, and a stream with none a crash, which fails in passing (decision a-crash-is-tried-again), its calls and compactions told as any try's; nothing is taken in from main or read of the branch for the round, so that what the try committed is the round's. Only a try whose program said none of these, having taken nothing in, is given again, told the same.
Why: Given again, a try was told the message its session already held: in work 4's trial the stopped try had read a file and tried an edit, the session held its 5,717-character message twice, and its agent, finding in the session its own return that never reached the engine, said the result of its tests had not come; read of the branch, the try's commit was written as the outside's. Giving the try again with what the session lacks was turned down: whether a program took its message in is said only as its try ends, which this try never did in an engine that saw it, and the cut would be hidden within the round where a failure shows it (principle nothing-passes-for-normal). The stream is written as it comes, so it holds what the program printed until its engine ended.

### decision a-shared-cause-is-one-wait
A round that fails for a cause outside every work and the same for all of them (the usage limit of the account in use, a command line that cannot run, the network down, the service unavailable) holds the rounds of every work from the first round that meets it, its own work's next round included, as one wait worked out from the records of every work; no work is paused for it, and every work goes on by itself once the wait is over. A usage limit is the account's own: it holds the rounds only while the account its round ran under is the one in use, and only a round under that account coming back ends it. The wait stands from the first such round after the last round that came back, and since when it stands is what the owner is told. A cause of one session or one model (a context past what the account may send without credits, a model the account may not use) pauses the work that meets it, with why; a model the service does not have pauses, with why, that work and every other whose rounds run on it.
Why: One cause stops every work alike: paused one by one, each would wait for the owner to start it by hand once the cause had passed, and a mark written on each would have to be lifted on each. A cause of one session or one model does not pass with time: held as every work's, it would stop works it does not touch and be tried again for ever. A model the service does not have fails every round on it until the installation names another, so each other work on it would fail its next round for the same cause.

### decision the-engine-checks-the-outside-itself
While the agents' wait stands, no round of any work tries whether its cause has passed: the engine checks the outside by itself, with no round and in no work's session, under the account in use, by the sign-in (`auth status`, in that account's directory of settings), then the network (a look at the service's host), then, once both are well, one short request on the cheapest level's model, in a session that is not kept, with the agents' command and environment and that directory. A check under another account counts for no wait, and after a take the checks under the new account are counted from the first. The first check is half a minute after the latest round that met the cause, each next one twice as long after the one before, up to five minutes, counted over the whole wait; at a usage limit whose reset the round or the latest check said, a reset just passed being now, none is taken before the reset. Once a check finds the outside well, every work goes on from where it was, the round that met the cause run again. What a check found is kept only in memory, and after a stop the outside is checked again at once.
Why: The command line writes a round's message into its session before it calls the service, and makes up a reply where it cannot call it ("Not logged in", "You've hit your weekly limit"): retrying rounds through an outage filled a session with the same input again and again, and rounds that tried now and then left a copy of the whole message each time, three in three tries, a dozen or more over an hour's outage with two works going. The check needs no work's context, and asks what every round needs in the order in which each makes the next worth asking. Spaced to five minutes, a cause that passed is found within five minutes, and checks that find it down cost nothing but a refused request; counted over the whole wait, a cause the check does not find, as a limit of one model alone, is met by the rounds no more often than the checks are taken. That the cheapest model answering says the service serves the others rests on judgment, and the bound on tries in a row covers where it does not (decision a-row-of-outside-failures-pauses-its-work).

### decision a-row-of-outside-failures-pauses-its-work
A run whose rounds failed for a cause outside every work more times in a row than a failure in passing is tried again, every kind of such cause counted together, has its work paused, with why; a round that came back, or the work let on, starts the row anew.
Why: A round after such a failure begins only once a check found the outside well, so a row of them says the rounds meet what the check does not find, each writing its message into its session again; unbounded, it would go on at the checks' spacing for as long as the cause lasts (principle no-unseen-spending-loop).

### decision a-wait-is-over-at-the-first-answer
The agents' wait is over once the service answers a round begun after the latest round that met its cause with a message that is no error, without waiting for that round's end; what answered is kept only in memory.
Why: The answer shows the outside serves, and a round may go on for tens of minutes after it; held until its end, a failure meanwhile would fall into a wait that was over, its checks spaced as though the outside had stayed down. Lost in a stop, the round is given again and answers again.

### decision the-wait-holds-what-its-cause-stops
There is a wait for each service the works reach: the agents' service, whose wait holds only the agents' rounds, and the host the acts reach, whose wait holds only the acts; nothing that comes of one service's rounds ends the other's. An act the host answered is of the kind the answer's status tells (a fault of the host's own or its limit on requests fails in passing, any other refusal pauses its work whatever its words, and so does what only a person mends); an act that got no answer and fails in the words of a failing network begins the acts' wait where a look at that host finds it gone and is otherwise tried again in passing, an act of a host no network reaches, as the local repository, never beginning one, and any other failure pauses its work.
Why: The engine's acts need no agent but reach their host (a push, a pull request, a merge), and failing there one by one they would each wait for the owner; held for the agents' service they would wait for nothing, and an act that comes back says nothing of the agents' service, nor an agent's round of the host. Read for words of the network, a refusal whose message names a timeout would be tried again, and a server's fault in words the list lacks, as GitHub's 500, would wait for the owner. A failure in other words would fail again.

### decision the-acts-host-is-tried-by-a-look
The acts' wait is tried by a look at their host every half minute, and is over once a look reaches it, every act then going on; no act is sent to try it, and no time ends it without a look.
Why: A look costs nothing, while an act may hold its round for long (the check waits for the full CI), every other act held behind it; ended by time alone, every act would meet the host still gone and fail again.

The backup of the records is held by neither this wait nor the owner's pause: see decision backup-is-the-engines-own-tick in backup.md.

### decision the-retry-wait-is-a-call
The wait before a round that failed in passing is tried again is a call of the run that the engine makes and the clock answers at its time, its end counted from the failed round's end.
Why: Deciding then needs no clock, a wait made is known as made after a stop, and the work shows what it waits for and until when as for any call; deciding with the time as an input was turned down, since a decision could then not be taken again from the records alone.

### decision an-unread-return-is-sent-back
A return that could not be read is sent back to the agent in its session, as any return that does not meet the form, and counts among the times the engine sent the cell back (the walk's decision the-same-sender-again).
Why: The agent's work is done and only its hand-in is wrong, so running the round from its start would pay for it twice. Repairing a text that is no JSON, in the engine or by a cheap model, was turned down: the agent that wrote it holds in its session what it meant, while a repair guesses at it (judgment, not measured).

### decision a-flagged-round-begins-a-new-session
A round the service's safety check flagged, or the run guard stopped, is given again at once in a new session of its line, whose name is worked out from how many of the line's rounds failed so; failed so again in the same run, with only failures between, the work is paused.
Why: The same session would give the check, or the guard, the same context again, more likely the cause than the task's words, though that is a judgment no case has shown; a second time says what the agent is given needs a look.

### decision a-note-run-is-held-only-by-its-own-pause
While a work is paused, a run that answers the owner, a note a person wrote to its cell or the owner turning to it, has its round after a failure held only where the engine paused the work since that round began, for its failure or for the budget it took the work over, which its carrier says before the round's end.
Why: A failure that is tried again by itself, or waits with every work, is no cause the owner must see first, and holding it would leave the note unanswered for as long as the owner's own pause lasts.
