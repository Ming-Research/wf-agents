- Messages are always asynchronous, with no waiting for a reply. What blocks is the problem itself, asking someone is
  one way to solve it, and the system blocks only where the problem is. With talk tied into the flow, some blocking and
  some not, a chain of questions may lose control of the state, and going back may not run through.

- A block can also be handed by one project's architect to another's, a hand-over one graph cannot draw, since projects
  may depend on each other.

- The owner's own matters keep direct channels: the owner can speak to an engineer; an engineer can tell the owner
  something one way, and put questions to the owner by their kind; an architect can ask what the owner left unclear, and
  ask approval of a policy draft.

- There is no mechanism against asking again what was answered: a round that can ask sees the questions open, and both
  sides can judge, so it does not go back and forth for ever. Such a mechanism would only waste tokens, as would a rule
  against asking twice sent with each batch.
