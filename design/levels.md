### decision a-level-is-a-difficulty
An item has one level, which says only how hard its work is and chooses the model its agents run on, never how hard each step thinks. A level's description speaks of difficulty alone.
Why: Levels that bind a model and an effort together were turned down: a step that only gathers needs less thought than one that designs, whatever the item, and an item's difficulty has nothing to do with that. Describing levels by whether decisions are needed misled whoever gave an item its level, since that is not difficulty.

### decision effort-is-the-flows
Every agent step names its reasoning effort in the flow's definition, which changes only by changing the flow; there is no setting of it on the page or in the installation. A step of searching or judging is not given the lowest effort, even on the cheapest model.
Why: An effort is a property of what the step does, which the flow says, and a setting beside the flow would let the two drift unseen; a step left to the program's own effort would run at whatever the program's default is, which nobody chose. The cheapest model at the lowest effort judged too poorly.

### decision only-deep-research-runs-above
The deep research runs one level above its item's; every other line, the reviews among them, runs at the item's own. No line runs above the highest level an item may be at, so that an item's work never reaches the top level when the installation keeps that out of items.
Why: A weaker agent doing the deep research may fix the surface and stop, hiding a larger problem that no later step finds, and few works take that way, so the higher model costs little in all. A review is a second pair of eyes after which the implementation is judged again, and it runs on far more works. The top level costs out of proportion to its gain for everyday work, which is why an installation may hold items below it.

### decision what-a-round-ran-on-is-kept-with-it
What each try of an agent's round ran on, its level, that level's model and its effort, is kept beside what the try was told, before it, and the past is read from that and never worked out again from the item's level, the flow and the settings. A try runs on the item's level and the settings as they are when it begins.
Why: The owner may put an item at another level, the settings may give a level another model, and the flow may raise a line or change an effort; a round read by today's would say it ran on what it did not. A record in the work's records was turned down: nothing decides by what a round ran on, and it is chosen by the one who runs it, as the message the round is told is, and kept where that message is.

### decision a-level-that-no-longer-holds
An item whose level the settings no longer have, or now hold above the highest, is not run at another level: its round fails, saying why, after any program left running for it is stopped, and the work waits for the owner. An item that has no level at all runs at the default and is shown as having none.
Why: Running an item quietly at a level other than its own would spend at a model nobody chose for it. A round that failed before stopping what was left running for it would leave that program spending and editing the worktree unseen. No level can be given to an item whose level was never kept without guessing.
