### principle owner-taste-judges
A design on the owner's page is judged by the owner's taste and use; user research and general design guidelines are no argument against them.
Why: wf-agents serves only its owner; research and guidelines are the grounds of products made for the public. (Article 8)

### principle scarce-emphasis
The strongest marks (a filled background, colour, motion) and the first places go to what matters most, weaker marks and later places to the rest; one meaning has one colour.
Why: Strong marks spent on small things leave nothing to make the important stand out; when everything stands out, nothing does.

### principle one-primary-command
A view has one primary command, the one it exists for. A command whose effect reaches beyond what its name says asks before it acts, saying what will happen.
Why: The primary button tells the owner what the view is for; the owner must know the consequences before they happen.

### principle room-goes-to-content
Room on the screen goes to content: a control or fact shares a row rather than taking one, and at every width the page fits the window, the least needed part giving way first.
Why: The chart and the lists need the height and the width; each extra row was taken from what it shows, a page that scrolled sideways hid its edge, and cells squeezed to one letter a line could not be read. (Article 8)

### principle owner-keeps-their-place
What the owner reads and acts on stays put: a control stands with what it governs, and nothing in view moves or changes unless the owner did it or the thing shown really changed.
Why: A control pressed from a view about something else did what the owner could not see; a list should not move under the owner's hand, and one that changes under the eyes cannot be read. (Article 8)

### principle typed-words-are-kept
Every box that takes text keeps what was typed and not sent, through leaving the page, opening something else, a
reload and the browser dropping the tab: it is written at each change, by the thing the box belongs to, and only text
is kept.
Why: Losing what the owner typed is a serious defect, and a browser that drops a tab does not promise to run anything
as the page leaves. Kept by where the box stands, one box would show another's words; and what is not text, such as
an option picked, could come back as something else unnoticed.

### principle given-and-returned-shown-whole
What a round was given and what it handed back are shown in their own structure: nothing translated, merged, picked
out or left out.
Why: What goes in and comes out has a structure of its own, and when the page chose what to show it left out things
of value.

### principle definitions-shown-as-written
What the flow's files define is shown by its key and its value as they are written there: the page puts no sentence
of its own in place of a key or a value, and holds no words of its own about what a definition holds. What a reader
needs to know of a thing is written in the files, with the thing.
Why: Sentences the page made up for each attribute could not be found in the files, said less than the key, and were
a second copy kept by hand in the code, which drifted from the definition.

### principle told-apart-by-shape
What the page says to the owner, what was sent to an agent and what an agent handed back each have one fixed look and
are told apart by shape. What was sent is shown as it was sent, in order, with no words of the page between, and a
notice of the page never looks like something an agent wrote.
Why: The owner took the first line sent to an agent for a title of the page, and could not tell what was sent from
what was shown. Shape tells them apart without reading, in either appearance and to eyes that do not tell colours; a
colour for each speaker was turned down, because the colours already mean other things (principle
colour-only-for-state).

### principle prose-in-the-page-font
What an agent or the owner wrote is shown in the page's font; only what is shown as it came (a message as sent, a
file, what a tool printed) stays in monospace.
Why: What they write is prose, and monospace makes prose slower to read.

### principle asked-where-it-arises
What the owner must answer or decide is shown and answered where it arises, in the text it is about, and stays there.
It is never gathered at the top or in a list of its own; elsewhere the page only counts it and points to it.
Why: A question is always about what stands around it: gathered elsewhere, the owner reads the matter in one place
and answers in another.

### principle what-an-agent-keeps-is-readable
What an agent keeps for its own use the owner can read, each earlier version of it and what changed.
Why: What an agent keeps for itself and the owner cannot see leaves the owner uneasy about it.

### principle asks-beside-what-it-is-about
Where the page asks before it acts, it asks in a line beside what the owner decides by, and not in a dialog laid over
it.
Why: The owner decides by what is shown there, and a dialog would cover it.

### principle a-control-says-what-it-does
A control says, when pointed at, what it does, and one that cannot be used now says why.
Why: Otherwise the owner learns what a control does only by pressing it, and cannot tell what would make a dead one
usable; this rests on judgment.

### principle every-mark-is-explained
Every extra mark on a chart or in a list (a badge, a weight, a colour, a word, the style of a line) is explained
where it is used, in a legend or on pointing at it.
Why: Without the words the owner cannot read the marks.

### principle what-opens-can-be-copied
What is drawn as a link and opens something on the page can be selected and copied as text.
Why: The owner copies what the page shows to say what is wrong, and a copied panel had lost every name of a round and
of a cell.

### principle the-head-says-what-holds-it
Where a work is both paused and waiting for the owner, its head says what really holds it.
Why: With one place saying it is paused and another that it waits for the owner, the owner cannot tell which of the
two stops it.

### principle one-catalogue-of-words
All the page's own words are in English and come from one catalogue.
Why: The owner reads the page in English, and one catalogue makes the name of each thing the same wherever the page
shows it.

### principle one-count-of-what-waits
The owner sees one count, of what waits on the owner: the questions that now hold a work. Only these notify, once as
each comes and again each hour while any is left; everything else is only marked as unread, and a question a work has
gone on past is shown as still to confirm and raises nothing until the merge waits for it.
Why: Several counts with different meanings did not agree with one another, and nobody trusted them.
