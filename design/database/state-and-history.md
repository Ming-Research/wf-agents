Decision: The current state the engine needs for its next step is kept in `works` and written in the same transaction as the round that changed it, while the history (`rounds`, `tool_runs`) is written once and never changed, and the summaries people and agents read are computed from the history, because the next step then never depends on recomputing the whole history, and the history stays the record of what happened.

Decision: The change records are written by triggers generated from `db/schema.sql`, for every table except the write-once history, instead of by each piece of code that writes, because no write can then forget to record itself, and the history tables are their own record.

Decision: The images the owner sends with words are kept in `attachments`, each with the words it came with, written once and never changed, and are not tracked in the change records, which keep a row as JSON and hold no bytes: who sent an image, and when, is the change that wrote its words, instead of a file kept beside the database, because the owner asked that what is sent go into the database, and an image is history as the words are.

Decision: One agent run from the input the engine gives to the return handed in is one round; the engine's own step, one run of a tool cell and one answer from the owner are each a round too, and a round that only answers an interjection on a line not running has no cell, because then everything a work went through is in one table, in order.

Decision: A round is known by its work and its number among the work's rounds, counted from 1 in the order they started, and named R<work>-<n> (R5-9 is the ninth round of work 5) wherever a person or an agent sees one: the page and its addresses, the interface, the command line, the views agents query and the addresses in agents' messages; the rounds' key is the work and the number, every row that points to a round holds both under a foreign key over the two, and no number across all works is left, instead of one sequence of round numbers across all works, because a person or an agent reading a round should see at once which round of its work it is, and one sequence across works made a work's rounds jump (work 1's 102 rounds were spread over 1 to 240).

Decision: The rounds recorded before they were named by their work were renumbered by a migration, in the order of their old numbers within each work, and every row that pointed to one points to it by its work and number; the texts already sent and kept, the rounds' records and the change records, stay as they were sent, so an address or a row in them names a round by its old number, instead of rewriting them into the new names, because a record is what was sent and is never changed.

Rejected:
- Keeping the number across all works as the rounds' key and adding each round's number in its work beside it: rejected because the global number would stay in every row that points to a round and in the views, and agents and the owner would keep meeting a second name for the same round.
